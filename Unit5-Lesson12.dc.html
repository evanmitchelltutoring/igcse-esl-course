A model essay · the target you're drafting toward

"A machine can never truly make art." How far do you agree?

Few questions divide the art world like this one: can a machine truly make art, or only imitate it? While AI can now produce images of startling beauty, I largely agree that a machine can never truly make art, because real art depends on something a program does not have — human intention.

There is no denying that AI produces work that moves people. It could be argued that if an image brings a viewer to tears, the question of who made it hardly matters. A machine can imitate almost any style, and some output is, by any fair measure, beautiful. To dismiss this would be dishonest.

And yet beauty alone is not art. A machine might copy the look of a painting, but it cannot mean it. It produces patterns it has learned, with no idea what they say or why. If there is no intention behind an image, this calls into question whether it can be called art at all, however authentic it appears.

My strongest reason, though, is simpler. What moves us in real art is knowing a person chose every line for a reason — that a human being decided this mattered enough to make. That choice is the merit of art, and it cannot be automated.

On balance, a machine may assist art, but it can never legitimately claim to have made it. The heart of the matter is not the image, but the mind behind it — and that, for now, remains ours alone.
