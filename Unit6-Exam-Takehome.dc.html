Way in — before you read

Most schools tell students exactly what to wear. One school tried the opposite — and the results surprised everyone, including the students who had asked for the change.

As you read, keep one question in mind: the experiment passed through four stages — can you name all four, and say what the writer thinks the school really learned?

The text — annotate in the margin

Paragraphs are numbered for the exam. Use the right-hand margin for your Meaning · Method · Response notes.

1  For as long as anyone could remember, the students of Northgate School had worn the same grey uniform, and for just as long they had complained about it. So when a new head teacher announced a one-year trial — students could wear whatever they liked — the cheer in the hall could be heard from the street. Freedom, at last. What followed was more complicated, and more interesting, than anyone expected.
Your notes ↓
2  The first weeks were a celebration. Corridors that had been a sea of grey filled with colour, and students spoke of how liberating it felt to dress as themselves. Teachers reported brighter moods and, for a while, few complaints. If the story had ended there, the trial would have been remembered as a simple triumph.
Your notes ↓
3  But by the second month, a quieter problem had surfaced. Some students began to dread mornings. Choosing an outfit every day, they said, had become a daily test they could not opt out of — and a visible one. A few admitted they now noticed exactly who could afford the newest trainers and who could not. The grey uniform, for all its dullness, had hidden those differences. Without it, a low-level anxiety crept back into the building.
Your notes ↓
4  So the school looked for a compromise. Rather than abandon the trial or simply return to grey, students and teachers designed something in between: a small set of relaxed rules. Any plain clothes were allowed, in any colour, but no visible brand names or logos. It was not the total freedom of the first week, nor the strict grey of before. It was, most agreed, a fairer middle.
Your notes ↓
5  The most surprising result came at the end of the year. When students were asked to vote on keeping the new rules or returning fully to the old uniform, a small majority voted, unexpectedly, to reintroduce a uniform — but one they had helped redesign. They had learned something the complaints had hidden: that a uniform was never only a cage. It was also, quietly, a kind of shelter.
Your notes ↓
6  The head teacher is careful not to claim she planned it this way. What the year taught, she says, was not that uniforms are good or bad, but that clothes are never a small matter in a school — they carry belonging, money, and mood all at once. The students did not simply get their freedom, or lose it. They came to understand, from the inside, what they had been arguing about all along.

Before you come to the exam, check you can…
  • name the four stages: freedom celebrated → a hidden problem → a compromise → the surprising vote;
  • explain why the mood changed in the second month;
  • say what the students learned a uniform also is ("a kind of shelter");
  • point to one line where you can hear the writer's own attitude.
