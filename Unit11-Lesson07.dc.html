Structure 1 · telling the story

Three tenses, three jobs

Past simple
the main events, one after another
She opened the door and stepped inside.
Past continuous
background action, often interrupted
Rain was falling as she watched.
Past perfect
something earlier than the main events
The villagers had vanished before the ship arrived.

Structure 2 · weighing the clues

Past deduction: modal + have + done

Choose the modal that matches how sure you are — from certain, to possible, to certainly not.
must have
I'm sure it's true
The cup was warm — someone must have just left.
might / could have
it's possible
They might have left by the back door.
can't have
I'm sure it's not true
The boats were tied up — they can't have left by sea.
Compare · weak vs strong

Same clues — sharper grammar

Weak
"The fire was not lit. Someone left in a hurry. They didn't leave by sea because the boats are there."
Flat past simple; no sequence; certainty not marked.
Strong
"The fire had been laid but never lit; someone must have risen in a hurry. They can't have left by sea, because the boats were still tied up."
Past perfect sets the sequence; must/can't have weigh the clues.

Try it · tense or modal?

Fill each gap with the best form

1. By the time the boat arrived, the keepers (vanish) ______. (earlier event)
2. She (stand) ______ at the window when she saw the van. (background)
3. The lock had no scratch, so the thief ______ have known the code. (you're sure)
4. We're not certain, but they ______ have fled overland. (possible)
5. His ticket was upstairs, so he ______ have been in the study. (sure it's not true)

Tense or modal?

1. …the keepers had vanished. (past perfect)
2. She was standing at the window… (past continuous)
3. …the thief must have known the code. (certain)
4. …they might / could have fled overland. (possible)
5. …he can't have been in the study. (certain not)
