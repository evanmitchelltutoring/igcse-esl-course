Your task

A neglected river runs through your town. A local group wants the council to pay for its restoration, and the council has asked residents to submit reports.

Write a report for the council: describe the problems, explain what could be done, and recommend action.

120–160 words formal & objective use the passive

The shape of a report · use headings

Problems → What could be done → Recommendation

Current problemsWhat is wrong now — pollution, neglect, lost wildlife.
What could be doneA series of steps, in the passive: "the water should be tested…"
RecommendationThe benefits, and a clear call for the council to act.
A model report · river restoration
Problems Steps Recommendation
Current problems
For years, the river has been polluted by waste from nearby factories, and much of its wildlife has disappeared. The banks have been neglected, and litter is often left there.
What could be done
Several steps could be taken. First, the water should be tested regularly and the results made public. Second, filters could be fitted at the factories, so that waste is no longer released. Finally, reeds could be planted and the old walls removed, so that wildlife can return.
Recommendation
It is recommended that the council fund this work. If the river were restored, wildlife would recover and the whole town would benefit. Action should be taken as soon as possible.

Writer's toolkit · report language

Language for a formal report

Section stems
For years the river has been ___.
Several steps could be ___.
First, the water should be ___.
It is recommended that ___.
Modal passives
could be taken · should be tested · can be protected
Linkers & vocab
as a result · so that · therefore

Weak → strong · lift your writing

Say the same thing — better

✗ Weak
“I think we should save the river.”
↓
✓ Strong
“Steps have been taken to restore the river, and further action is needed.”
Avoid these
  • “I think” / “I feel”
  • Emotional words
  • Active where the passive is more objective

Capstone project · now you're ready

Protect a Living Thing

In your group, choose a plant, animal or wild place that matters. Research it, then build a short campaign — 3–5 slides or one page — that persuades others to help protect it, and present to the class.

What & where

Who can help

What can ordinary people do?
