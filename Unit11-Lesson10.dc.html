A report for the local newspaper

Strange lights and noises have been reported at an old building in your area, and some people are worried. Your local newspaper has asked you to look into it and report what is actually known. Write a report of 150–200 words.
1 · describe what people claim they saw or heard

Plan · copy the frame

Four short sections, with headings

Introduction
One line: what this report is about. ~20 words.
What people claim
The reports, attributed — "residents say…". ~50 words.
How reliable it is
Weigh it; give an ordinary explanation. ~60 words.
Conclusion
What is, and isn't, known. Don't overclaim. ~30 words.
A model · learn the shape, don't copy it

"The Reported Lights over Hillford"

Introduction. This report examines the strange lights seen above Hillford last month and considers how reliable the accounts are.

What people claim. Several residents reported bright, silent lights moving in formation for about ten minutes. One witness described them as perfectly still before they shot away; another claimed the air felt cold.

How reliable the reports are. The testimony should be treated with caution. Most witnesses saw the lights at night and from a distance, so they might have misjudged the height and speed. There is no photograph or recording. The lights can't have been ordinary aircraft, according to locals, but they may well have been drones or a weather balloon.

Conclusion. On the available evidence, no firm explanation can be proven. The event was unusual, but it must have had a natural cause; we simply cannot yet say what it was.

Notice → attributed ("reported", "claimed"), hedged ("might have", "may well have"), and honest at the end ("cannot yet say").

Language to reach for
  • reported · claimed · according to

  • can't have · must have had (certain)
