Insert — the text

Read the whole text once before you begin. Paragraphs are numbered.

1  When the last wolves were hunted out of Yellowstone National Park in the 1920s, few people thought it mattered. The park looked beautiful without them. Yet over the following seventy years, scientists watched something strange and worrying happen to the whole landscape.

2  With no wolves to hunt them, the number of elk grew enormously. These large deer ate young trees faster than the trees could grow. Along the rivers, the willows and aspens that once lined the banks almost disappeared. Without those trees, songbirds lost their nesting places, and beavers, which need willow to build with, became rare.

3  In 1995, after years of debate, scientists released fourteen wolves back into the park. What followed surprised even the experts. The wolves, of course, killed some elk, but the bigger effect was on how the elk behaved. The deer no longer dared to linger in the open river valleys where they could be caught. They began to move more, and to avoid the most dangerous places altogether.

4  Freed from constant grazing, the young trees finally grew tall. As the willows returned, so did the songbirds. Beavers came back too, and their dams created ponds that gave homes to fish, frogs and insects. Even the rivers changed shape: the new plants held the banks together, so the water ran in steadier channels instead of washing the soil away.

5  Scientists call this a "trophic cascade" — a chain of effects that begins with one species at the top and ripples all the way down. The story of Yellowstone taught ecologists an important lesson: a single missing animal can quietly change an entire ecosystem, and bringing it back can heal far more than anyone expects.

Section D — The writer's attitude (8 marks)
Plan first. Which words in the text show the writer's opinion, not just facts? Jot two here before you write.
[2]D1. In paragraph 1 the writer calls the change "strange and worrying." What does this tell you about how the writer feels about losing the wolves?
[2]D2. In paragraph 5 the writer says one missing animal can "quietly change an entire ecosystem." What does the word quietly suggest about how the damage happened?
[4]D3. Does the writer think bringing the wolves back was a good thing? Explain your answer, using two pieces of evidence from the text.
Marking — how your answers are judged
BandWhat it looks like
StrongAll factual answers correct and precise. For attitude questions, you identify the writer's opinion and support it with the exact words that carry it, explaining their effect.

EAL note: You are marked on understanding, not perfect English. A correct idea in simple, clear words earns full marks. Use the sentence stems from class — "The writer feels… because the word ___ suggests…" — to show your thinking.
