The rule · copy this into your notes

Two tools: predict the future, urge a priority

Future forms (predict)
will — a belief: Costs will fall.
going to — evidence now: Prices are going to drop; three firms just cut them.
might / could — less sure: We might reach Mars by 2050.
Necessity modals (urge)
must / have to — strong: We must spend wisely.
should / ought to — advice: Governments should share the cost.
mustn't = forbidden · don't have to = not required.
Watch it: base form after a modal ("we must to spend" → "we must spend") · mustn't ≠ don't have to — one forbids, one just isn't required.
show me
See the lift · weak vs strong

Same idea — flat, then sharp

Weak
"In the future space is cheap. We spend money well. Space is good for everyone."
Flat present tense, no prediction, no urging.
Strong
"Costs will keep falling, so within decades space travel could reach ordinary people. But governments must spend wisely, and companies should share the burden."
A graded prediction + two necessity modals.

Practice 1 · spot the error

Find and fix one mistake in each

1. Governments must to spend the money wisely.
2. By 2040, prices will fell a lot.
3. We don't have to waste money — it is forbidden.
4. Look at the new rockets — space travel will get cheaper very soon, definitely.
5. Scientists think we will might reach Mars one day.
6. Companies should to share the cost.
Support → after a modal, use the base form (no to, no past).
Stretch → which sentence should use going to, not will? Why?
Answer · mark your own

Find and fix

1. Governments must spend the money wisely. (base form after a modal)
2. By 2040, prices will fall a lot. (will + base form)
3. We mustn't waste money — it is forbidden. (forbidden = mustn't)
4. Look at the new rockets — space travel is going to get cheaper. (evidence now)
5. Scientists think we might reach Mars one day. (one modal only)
6. Companies should share the cost. (no to after should)
