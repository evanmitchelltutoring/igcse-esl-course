Clinic 1 · Note-taking during playback

Capture without losing the thread

The trap
Writing a full sentence while the recording moves on — so you miss the next answer trying to finish the last. The audio does not wait.
The fix · notes, not sentences
Jot only content words — nouns, numbers, names. Abbreviate ("b/c", "→", "£"). You have two hearings: catch the gist first time, fill detail the second.
Leave a blank rather than freeze. A gap you return to on the second listen beats three missed answers.
Rep · four short recordings · play it twice

Note only the content word that answers each

1. What time does the library close today? ______
2. What should players bring to the sports hall? ______
3. What time does the number 42 bus now leave? ______
4. Where does the guided tour start? ______
Answers: 1 · three o'clock · 2 · trainers (not boots) · 3 · twenty past · 4 · the courtyard. Notice each one corrects an expected answer — that's the next clinic.
Clinic 2 · Gap-fill traps

The obvious word is often wrong

The trap
Writing the first number or word you hear. Speakers correct themselves: "the letter said eight thirty — but it's actually eight fifteen." The first figure is the decoy.
The fix · wait for the correction
Hear the whole sentence before you write. Listen for "not… but…", "actually", "changed to", "instead of" — the real answer usually comes after them.
Signal words that flag a correction coming: actually · in fact · rather than · now · instead · sorry, I mean.
Rep · complete the notes · play it twice

Beware the first number you hear

Coach leaves the car park at __________
Bring a __________ because the café is busy
Money for the entrance? __________
Back at school by __________
Answers: eight fifteen (not eight thirty) · packed lunch · not needed / already paid · half past four (not four o'clock). The letter's times were the traps.
Clinic 3 · Prediction before listening

Use the pause — don't waste it

The trap
Sitting idle in the pause before playback, then meeting every question cold — reading and listening at the same time, and doing neither well.
The fix · predict the answer type
In the pause, read every question and mark what each gap needs: a number? a place? a name? a time? Then you're listening for something, not just listening.
"Collected every ______" almost certainly needs a frequency (weekly, fortnightly). Predicting the type filters out the noise.
Rep · predict first, then listen · recycling scheme

Mark the answer TYPE, then fill it

Scheme covers about ______ homes  (type: ____)
Paper and card go in the ______ box  (type: ____)
Food waste is collected ______  (type: ____)
The biggest mistake is ______ in with the paper
Answers: twelve thousand (number) · blue (colour) · every week / weekly (frequency) · greasy pizza boxes. Predicting "number / colour / frequency" told your ear exactly what to grab.
Clinic 4 · Transcription & spelling

A right answer, spelled wrong, scores zero

The trap
Hearing the answer correctly but writing it wrong — a misspelled key word, a missing plural -s, or a homophone (there/their). If the word is unclear, the mark is lost.
The fix · transcribe carefully
When a name is spelled out, write it letter by letter as you hear it ("R-A-M-A-N"). Check plurals, capital letters, and easy-to-confuse spellings on the second listen.
Number words trip people too: fourteen vs forty, fifteen vs fifty. Listen for the stress.
Rep · transcribe exactly · play it twice

Spell it right — the mark depends on it

The speaker's surname is __________  (spelled aloud)
Her first job was in a __________
She started at age __________
She now manages __________ staff
Answers: Raman · hotel kitchen · fourteen (14, not 40) · forty (40, not 14). The name mark needs the exact spelling; the number marks need the right one of the pair.
Clinic 5 · Long audio, many questions

Don't lose your place

The trap
In a long interview, falling behind — still writing Q3 while the speaker answers Q4, then filling Q3 with the wrong information.
The fix · questions follow the audio
Questions come in the recording's order. Keep a finger on the current question; when the topic shifts, move down. Use the interviewer's new question as your signpost.
If you miss one, leave it blank and jump to the next — chasing a lost answer costs you the two that follow.
Rep · follow in order · sea-turtle rescue interview

Keep pace — the questions track the answers

1. What was Nadia's job before the rescue centre? ______
2. What is the most common injury? ______
3. How long might a turtle that lost a flipper stay? ______
4. Her advice to someone who wants to help? ______
Answers: 1 · diving instructor · 2 · old fishing line / netting (~7 in 10) · 3 · eighteen months · 4 · pick up the line on the beach (don't just send money). Each interviewer question was your signpost to move down.
