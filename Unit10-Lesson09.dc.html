Decode the task · before you write

The task, in plain words

Your school magazine runs a feature, 'A Changing World'. Write an article (150–200 words) about one global issue and what young people can do about it.
Why it matters
To people your age — make it personal and close.
What to do
One or two doable actions — not "save the world".
Get involved
Encourage warmly — a call to action, not a lecture.

The shape · four beats

Headline → Hook → Why + What → Call to action

Headline
Short and catchy — a question or a little wordplay.
Hook
A question, fact or tiny scene; speak to the reader directly.
Why + What
Why it matters to readers your age, then one or two real actions.
Call to action
Imperatives: "Start small. Bring a friend. Don't wait."

Weak → strong · lift the register

Preachy, then lively

Weak (preachy)
"Everybody must care about the environment. It is very important. People should not waste things. This is a serious issue."
Lecturing, vague, no reader, no real action.
Strong (lively)
"Ever wondered what one person can really change? More than you'd think. Swap fast fashion for second-hand — start with one item this week."
Reader address, energy, an imperative.

A model article · watch the four beats

"Fast Fashion: A Problem We Can Wear Out"

Every week, new clothes appear at prices almost too cheap to believe. To people my age, fast fashion feels exciting and harmless.

In reality it is one of the most polluting industries on the planet, and it should matter to us, because it is our generation that will inherit the damage — mountains of waste, rivers dyed with chemicals.

The good news is we have real power. First, rethink your consumption: buy less, choose quality, repair what you own. Second, swap or buy second-hand with friends. We can also demand accountability from the companies that profit from waste.

So make a commitment today. Collaborate with your classmates, share what you learn, and feel the empowerment of acting together. Change starts with what we choose to wear.
