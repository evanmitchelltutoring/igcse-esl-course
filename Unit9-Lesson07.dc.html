The rule · copy this into your notes

The verb before it decides: -ing or to

verb + -ing (gerund)
enjoy · avoid · keep · finish · suggest · mind
I enjoy learning.
verb + to (infinitive)
decide · want · hope · choose · need · learn
I decided to retrain.
preposition + -ing
good at · interested in · without · before
good at solving problems.
Watch it: after a preposition it's always -ing ("good at solve → solving") · to here is a preposition too: "I look forward to learning", not "to learn".

Same idea — wrong pattern, then right

Weak
"I enjoy to learn languages. I'm good at learn them, and I've decided learning Spanish."
Three pattern errors — jarring to read.
Strong
"I enjoy learning languages. I'm good at learning them, and I've decided to learn Spanish."
enjoy + -ing · at + -ing · decide + to.

Answer · mark your own

Find and fix

1. I want to learn a new skill. (want + to)
2. She's interested in learning how to code. (in + -ing)
3. Keep practising and you'll improve. (keep + -ing)
4. He avoided making the same mistake. (avoid + -ing)
5. I look forward to learning from you. (to = preposition → -ing)
6. We decided to study together. (decide + to)
