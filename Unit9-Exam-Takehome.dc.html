Way in — before you read

We often assume learning belongs to the young. These four adults — aged from their forties to their sixties — disagree. Each finally learned something they had put off, and each found the late start was no barrier at all. As you read, notice why each one succeeded.

Predict: which of the four do you think found it hardest to begin — and why?

The text — annotate in the margin

Accounts are labelled A–D for the exam. Use the right-hand margin for your Gist · Detail · Match notes.

A — Hassan
I learned to swim at forty-five. As a child I had been afraid of water after a bad experience, and the fear followed me into adulthood. Booking that first lesson was the bravest thing I have ever done. My teacher was endlessly patient and never once made me feel foolish. Now I swim every week, and the fear is simply gone.

B — Greta
I taught myself to code using free videos online. I have a full-time job and two children, so I studied late at night when the house was quiet. Nobody pushed me; the discipline had to come entirely from me. It took two years, but last month I was offered a new job because of the skill — proof that the late nights were worth it.
Your notes ↓
C — Tomas
I started learning the violin at sixty, just for the joy of it. I will never play in an orchestra, and I do not care. People assume you learn an instrument to perform, but I do it purely for the pleasure of the sound. My grandchildren laugh at the squeaks, but they also clap when I get something right.
Your notes ↓
D — Priya
I went back to study for a university degree in my fifties, sitting in classes beside students younger than my own daughter. At first I felt I did not belong. But I soon realised that my years of life experience were an advantage, not an embarrassment — I understood the books in a way the younger students could not.

Before you come to the exam, check you can…
  • say in one phrase what each person learned: Hassan → swimming; Greta → coding; Tomas → violin; Priya → a degree;
  • name what made each one different — a fear overcome, self-discipline, learning for joy, life experience as an advantage;
  • explain why Greta says "the discipline had to come entirely from me";
  • point to the line where Priya changes her mind about belonging.
