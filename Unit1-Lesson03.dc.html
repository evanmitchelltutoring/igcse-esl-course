My Corner

Reading · extended

There is a small, slightly crooked table in the back-left corner of the café on Soi 7, and for almost a year now it has been mine.

In the morning the place is pale and sleepy. Thin grey light leans through the window, the espresso machine clears its throat, and the only sound is the soft tick of someone's laptop keys. By four o'clock everything has changed. The narrow room fills with the warm smell of toasted bread, the low hum of a dozen overlapping conversations, and a golden, dusty light that makes even the chipped cups look beautiful. I like it best then.

My corner has a wobble in one leg and a long pale scar across the wood, where someone years ago pressed too hard with a pen. The seat is too low and the cushion has gone flat. None of that matters. From there I can see the door, the street, and the old woman who sells flower garlands on the pavement opposite — and nobody can see my screen.

I come here to read, to wait, to do nothing in particular. The owner no longer asks what I want; he simply nods, and a few minutes later a small, strong, sweetened coffee appears at my elbow. I have never told him my name. He has never told me his. And yet, sliding into that uneven chair with the afternoon noise rising around me, I feel something I never feel at home or at school: I feel, completely and quietly, that I belong here.

One good reading · yours may differ

Sample annotation

Meaning — The narrator and the owner are strangers, yet the narrator still feels they belong.
Method — "And yet…" turns the sentence; "completely and quietly" makes the belonging feel calm and certain.
Response — It's moving: you can belong somewhere without owning it, or even being known.

Reading for feeling · answer with evidence

Four questions

1. Why does the writer describe the dull morning before the golden afternoon?
2. The table is broken and the seat is too low. How does the writer feel about this? Which words tell you?
3. Find one detail that shows the writer feels safe or unwatched.
4. In your own words, how does the writer feel about the café overall?
Stuck? → name the feeling first, then hunt for the word that proves it.
Push further → find two different words that point to the same feeling.
Support → Look in the last paragraph. Start: “The narrator feels they ___.”
Stretch → Which single word makes the belonging feel calm and certain?
Answers · accept well-evidenced variations

Check your thinking

1. The dull morning makes the golden afternoon feel warmer and more special by contrast.
2. It doesn't bother the writer — "None of that matters." The faults make the corner feel loved, not perfect.
3. "nobody can see my screen" — the corner gives privacy and a sense of safety.
4. The writer loves it; it is a place where, quietly, they feel they belong.

Read for feeling — name it, prove it
Writers show feelings through details, not by naming them. Ask: what feeling? which word shows it? how does it make me react? (Meaning · Method · Response.)
