Decode the task · before you write

Describe a person you know through the way they dress.

Someone whose style says something about who they are. Build it Scene → Detail → Verdict, and let the reader picture them. 120–160 words.
1 · Scene
Who they are + the overall impression, in a rich opening.
2 · Detail
2–3 precise things they wear and how — shown, not listed.
3 · Verdict
What their style reveals — why it stays with you.

The craft · show, don't tell

Let the detail do the work

✗ Telling
"She is really stylish and different."
✓ Showing
"She arrives in a paint-flecked denim jacket two sizes too big, sleeves shoved to the elbow."
Expand the noun phrase, adjectives in order: opinion → size → age → colour → material → noun.
a lovely, worn, brown leather bag  ·  a bold little vintage badge — never "a leather brown lovely bag".
notebook
Weak → strong · upgrade the line

Turn a flat line into a vivid one

Weak: "He wears cool clothes and looks good."
Strong: "He favours a single, understated palette — charcoal, sand, off-white — pressed so sharply the creases could cut."
What changed: vague praise → concrete colours + one vivid image ("creases could cut"); "cool/good" cut entirely.
Avoid these
Telling words (nice, cool, stylish) with no detail · a flat list of garments · adjectives out of order · describing clothes but never the person — always land a verdict.

A model description · watch the three beats

"My aunt Rosa"

My aunt Rosa has never owned a plain thing in her life. She enters a room the way weather does — you feel her before you see her, all colour and clatter and warmth. Her style is loud, but never careless.

Today she is wearing a huge, hand-knitted crimson cardigan over a dress patterned with lemons, and rings on nearly every finger. Silver bangles slide and chime when she talks. Nothing quite matches, yet somehow it all belongs — a signature chaos only she could assemble.

Her clothes are pure self-expression: they announce a woman who refuses to shrink or apologise. Long after she has left, the room feels quieter and greyer — and you understand that her identity was never in the fabric, but in the bold nerve to wear it.
