The Real Price of Cheap Clothes

Mark problems & solutions

A T-shirt can now cost less than a cup of coffee. To a shopper, this looks like good news. To the planet, and to the people who make our clothes, it is a warning sign.

The system behind these low prices is called fast fashion. Brands copy the latest styles, manufacture them at enormous speed, and sell them cheaply, knowing buyers will return within weeks for the next look. To keep prices down, much of this clothing is mass-produced in factories where workers are paid very little. Reports have repeatedly described long hours, unsafe buildings, and wages too low to live on. The cheapness we enjoy in the shop is partly paid for by someone else, somewhere we never see.

The environmental cost is just as serious. Making clothes uses huge amounts of water and energy, and the dyes often pollute rivers. Worse, fast fashion is designed to be temporary. Garments are made to fall out of style — and sometimes to fall apart — within a season, so we keep buying. The result is mountains of waste. A great deal of discarded clothing is burned or buried, and synthetic fabrics can take centuries to break down.

Defenders of fast fashion make two arguments. First, it makes style affordable: a teenager on a small budget can dress well without spending a fortune. Second, the industry provides jobs in countries that need them. Both points are true, and they should not be dismissed. Cheap clothing is not simply evil, and the workers who depend on these jobs would suffer if every factory closed overnight.

But affordability and jobs do not require a system built on waste and low pay. The problem is not that clothes are cheap; it is that the true costs are hidden and pushed onto others. There are better paths. Buying fewer, better-made items reduces waste. Choosing second-hand keeps clothing in use for longer. Supporting brands that pay fairly and produce less rewards the right behaviour. None of these alone will fix the industry, but together they shift the pressure.

Solutions
