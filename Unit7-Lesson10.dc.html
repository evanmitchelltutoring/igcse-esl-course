Decode the task · before you write

"Esports is a real sport." How far do you agree?

"How far do you agree?" wants a clear position, the other side weighed fairly, and a verdict at the end. Use the unit's four tests as your evidence. 120–160 words.
1 · Take a position
Say where you stand in the first sentence — don't sit on the fence.
2 · Weigh both sides
Concede the strongest objection, then reason past it with evidence.
3 · Land a verdict
End by resolving the stance — with a reason, not a shrug.
notebook
The shape · five beats

Stance → concession → reasoning → your point → verdict

Stance — where you stand, in one clear sentence. "…on balance I believe it qualifies."
Concession — grant the best objection. "While critics argue… there is no denying that…"
Reasoning — turn it, backed by evidence. "Yet the evidence suggests this is only one criterion of four…"
Your point — your strongest independent argument.
Verdict — resolve it. "On balance, then, there is a strong case that…"
notebook
Weak → strong · upgrade the claim

Turn an assertion into a reasoned claim

Weak: "Esports is obviously a sport because loads of people play it."
Strong: "While popularity alone proves little, esports meets three of the four tests — organised competition, governing bodies and skill — which is a strong case."
What changed: the bare assertion becomes a reasoned claim — it concedes ("popularity proves little"), then cites the tests as evidence.
Avoid these
Sitting on the fence (no clear stance) · asserting with no evidence · forgetting to concede the other side · a conclusion that just repeats the question instead of resolving it.

A model essay · watch the five beats

"Esports is a real sport" — how far do you agree?

Whether esports is a "real sport" comes down to how we weigh four tests — and on balance I believe it qualifies.

While critics argue that a true sport must exhaust the whole body, there is no denying that gaming lacks the large-scale exertion of running or swimming.

Yet the evidence suggests this is only one criterion of four. Esports has organised competition, governing bodies and undeniable skill; and the body is not idle — elite players suffer genuine repetitive strain.

If physical exhaustion were the only test, darts and shooting would fail it too — yet we call them sports.

On balance, then, there is a strong case that esports is a sport — because it meets the tests we already use.
