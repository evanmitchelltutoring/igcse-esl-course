Two Languages, One Self · an interview with Lina

Read · then read again

Q: Does one language feel more 'you' than the other?

Lina: People expect me to choose, but I cannot. In one language I am louder, quicker to joke. In the other I am gentler — it is the language I say sorry in. They are both me. I am just a slightly different me in each.

Q: Do you switch without noticing?

Lina: All the time. At home, a sentence might start in one language and finish in the other. My grandmother calls it 'making a mess'. I call it normal. The strange part is doing it instinctively — my mouth chooses before my mind does.

Q: Has it ever caused trouble?

Lina: Small things. A word that is polite in one language sounds rude in the other if you translate it directly. I have apologised for jokes that were never insults. But the harder part is not the words. It is that some people think you must belong fully to one side or the other. I have spent years learning that the in-between is a real place to stand — not a failure to land.

Q: What do people misunderstand?

Lina: That it must be confusing all the time. Mostly it is not — it is ordinary to me. What is hard is the assumption that I am half of two things instead of fully something new. I am not fifty per cent one and fifty per cent another. I am one hundred per cent in-between, and that turned out to be its own whole thing.

Q: What would you tell a younger you?

Lina: That the discomfort fades. For years I tried to pick a side to make other people comfortable. Now I let the two languages sit in the same sentence and I do not apologise for it. The mess, it turns out, was never a mess. It was just me.
