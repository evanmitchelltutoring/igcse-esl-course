1 · Vocabulary reference

The unit's words, grouped in four families. Say them, then use them in your writing and speaking.

The Tourist Gaze
itinerary — a planned route or schedule for a journey
landmark — a famous, easily recognised feature of a place
picturesque — visually charming, especially prettily old
superficial — only on the surface; not deep
fleeting — lasting only a very short time
observe — to watch carefully in order to notice details
souvenir — an object kept as a reminder of a place visited
vantage point — a position giving a good, wide view
Living Elsewhere
settle in — to grow comfortable in a new place
unfamiliar — not known or recognised; strange to you
routine — a regular, fixed way of doing things
gradually — slowly, in small stages over time
acquaintance — someone you know slightly, not a close friend
friction — disagreement or tension between people
household — all the people who live in one home
inconvenience — minor trouble or difficulty
Code-Switching
bilingual — able to speak two languages well
fluent — able to speak a language smoothly and easily
dialect — a regional form of a language
accent — how someone pronounces words, showing origin
adjust — to change slightly to fit a new situation
in-between — belonging fully to neither side
instinctively — without thinking, by natural habit
custom — a traditional way of behaving in a society
What Stays With You
nostalgia — a warm, slightly sad feeling for the past
homesick — sad because you are away from home
roots — the place or culture you come from
cherish — to care for and value deeply
memento — an object kept because it reminds you
perspective — a particular way of seeing something
resonate — to have a deep, lasting effect on someone
enduring — lasting a long time; not fading
Word families — learn one, gain three

One root grows into several. Learn the family and you always have the right form for a sentence.

reflect → reflection → reflective
adapt → adaptation → adaptable
observe → observation → observant
First-language bridge

In your first language, note what these key ideas mean — it helps them stick: belonging · code-switching · nostalgia.

5 · Grammar (Use of English)

5a · Present perfect (have/has + past participle)
  1. I have ____ (visit) three countries.
  2. She has lived in Tokyo ____ (for / since) two years.
  3. Have you ____ (ever) been to a festival abroad?
  4. They have ____ (know) each other since 2019.
5b · Defining relative clauses (who / which / that / where)
  1. She is the guide ____ showed us the temple.
  2. That is the village ____ my grandparents live.
  3. Sushi is a dish ____ comes from Japan.
  4. Combine: "This is the café. We met there."

6 · Writing

6a · A magazine article — "Lessons from Away" (150–200 words)
Shape: Experience → Reflection → Resonance.   Stems: "I have lived / stayed in…" · "What surprised me was…" · "It taught me that…" · "Even now, when… I am back there for a moment." Use the present perfect and 5+ target words (belonging · perspective · adapt · routine · nostalgia · home).
6b · A description — "A Place That Shaped You" (150–200 words)
Shape: Scene → Detail → Verdict.   Show, don't tell: give details the reader can sense (a smell, a sound, one cracked tile), not adjectives that tell. Use a defining relative clause and the present perfect; end on what the place made of you.
