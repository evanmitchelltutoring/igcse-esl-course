Small Changes, Big Differences

Four people · one habit each
A — Tom

I used to drink two or three energy drinks a day, especially before exams, convinced they kept me alert. The truth was the opposite: I was wired, then exhausted. I quit completely in January. The first few days were rough, and I still miss the taste, but my sleep is steadier and I am no longer spending half my allowance on cans. I hadn't realised how much they were costing me until I stopped.

B — Lena

I swapped the bus for my bike. It started as a way to avoid the crowded morning route, but it has become the best part of my day. I arrive at school properly awake instead of half asleep, and after a few months I noticed I could climb the stairs without losing my breath. I never once thought of it as 'training'. The only real drawback is the rain, which can turn a cheerful ride into a miserable one.

C — Idris

Before big swimming competitions I used to lie awake, replaying everything that could go wrong. A coach suggested writing for five minutes each night — just my worries, nothing neat. I was sceptical, and honestly I forgot to do it for the first fortnight. Once it became a habit, though, something shifted. Putting a fear into words somehow made it smaller. I still get nervous, but the panic no longer controls me.

D — Mei

Joining the Saturday cooking club at our community centre was meant to be a one-off. Two years later I am still there. I learned to turn cheap ingredients into proper, balanced meals, which has genuinely changed how I eat. But what surprised me was how much the company mattered — laughing with the same group every week was as good for me as the food. These days I cook dinner for my whole family most evenings.

Listening · Part 5 · interview with a sleep scientist

Choose A, B or C — listen for the opinion

Your teacher reads the interview twice. Read all four questions before you listen.

1 · Why do teenagers struggle to sleep early?
A they're lazy  ·  B their body clock shifts later  ·  C they drink too much caffeine
2 · What does Dr Park say IS within their control?
A the body clock  ·  B school start times  ·  C what they do late in the evening
3 · Her view on studying very late?
A always useful  ·  B past a point, a tired brain absorbs little  ·  C the best time to revise
4 · Her single piece of advice?
A fix everything at once  ·  B move the phone out of the bedroom  ·  C never use a phone
Answer · mark your own

The interview — the answers

1 — B. "the body clock genuinely shifts later… their biology is working against an early bedtime."
2 — C. "what they do in that late evening certainly is within their control."
3 — B. "a tired brain stops absorbing new information… working hard to make themselves perform worse."
4 — B. "just move your phone out of the bedroom… it removes the temptation."

So the Do-Now myth is false — past a point, the extra hour costs more than it gives.
