This packet goes with the twelve lessons of Unit 9. Keep it in your folder and bring it to every lesson. It holds your vocabulary, retrieval starters, reading and grammar practice, writing frames, homework and your "I can…" self-check. Answers are given in class so you can mark your own work.

Three routines to remember
  • Add "yet": a growth mindset turns "I can't" into "I can't yet" — a wall into a step.
  • Learn the family + a partner word: not adapt alone but adaptable / adaptability, and a growth mindset.
  • The Skill Bank: one page in your notebook that grows every lesson — your own revision sheet.

1 · Vocabulary reference

The unit's words, grouped in four strands. Say them, then use them in your writing and speaking.

Teaching yourself
acquire — to gain a skill or knowledge through effort over time
autonomous — able to act and decide independently
motivation — the reason or desire that makes you want to do something
proficiency — a high level of skill or competence
resourceful — good at finding clever ways to solve problems
self-directed — organised by the learner, not a teacher
tutorial — a lesson, often online, that teaches one skill step by step
A changing world
adaptable — able to change easily to fit new conditions
competent — having enough skill to do something well
evolve — to develop gradually over time
lifelong — lasting your whole life
mindset — a way of thinking about something
obsolete — no longer useful; replaced by something newer
potential — abilities that may develop and lead to success
upskill — to learn new or better skills, especially for work
Learning from mistakes
analyse — to examine something in detail to understand it
constructive — helpful; meant to improve something
determination — firmness of purpose; not giving up
feedback — information on how well you are doing, so you can improve
overcome — to deal successfully with a problem
perseverance — continued effort despite difficulty
reflection — careful thought about what you could do better
self-discipline — making yourself do what you should
Mentors & communities
collaborate — to work together with others
encouragement — words that give someone confidence to continue
engagement — being involved and interested in an activity
expertise — expert skill or knowledge in an area
guidance — advice and help from someone more experienced
inspire — to make someone want to do something
mentor — an experienced person who advises you over time
peer — a person of the same age or level as you
Argument chunks — for the essay & debate
a growth mindset — belief that ability improves with effort
skills become obsolete — old skills stop being useful
stay relevant — keep your skills useful and current
reach our potential — become as capable as we can be
I take your point, but… — concede, then answer
Word families — learn one, gain three

One root grows into several. Learn the family and you always have the right form for a sentence.

adapt → adaptable → adaptability
motivate → motivation → motivated
reflect → reflection → reflective
First-language bridge

In your first language, note what these key ideas mean — it helps them stick: self-directed · obsolete · perseverance.

5 · Grammar (Use of English)

5a · Gerunds vs. infinitives

enjoy/avoid/keep + -ing · decide/want/hope + to · after any preposition, always -ing.

  1. Fix: "I want learning a new skill."
  2. Choose: "She's interested in (learn / learning) to code." __________
  3. Complete: "I look forward ___ (learn) from you." __________
5b · Present perfect continuous

have/has + been + -ing; for + a length, since + a start point.

  1. Fix: "I have studying French for two years."
  2. Choose: "She has been practising (for / since) three months." __________
  3. Build: (we / practise / together / weeks)

6 · Writing

6a · An informal email — a study spot I found (120–160 words)
Shape: Greeting → the spot (why you like it) → how it's helped → invite with a when → warm sign-off.   Friendly register; a gerund ("I love working there") and a present perfect continuous ("I've been studying there"); target words (accessible · motivation · self-directed · supportive · milestone).
6b · An argumentative essay — "Everyone should keep learning for life." (120–160 words)
Shape: Position → 2× P-R-R-E (Point → Reason → Result → Example) → Conclusion that looks forward.   Hold one position; use a gerund and a present perfect continuous; target phrases (stay relevant · skills become obsolete · adapt to a changing world · a growth mindset · reach our potential).
