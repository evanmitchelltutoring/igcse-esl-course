The idea · copy this into your notes

Keep the clue apart from the guess

An investigator separates what they know from what they think. A clue is a fact you can point to. A theory is an idea about the facts. Muddle the two and you believe anything.
A clue (evidence)
"The coffee cup was still warm." A fact anyone could check.
A theory (a guess)
"So someone was here minutes ago." An idea that fits — until better evidence appears.

Six phrases for reasoning about a mystery

the evidence suggests
base a claim on facts
it seems likely that
a careful, hedged guess
that would explain
link a theory to a clue
there's no proof that
flag a claim with no evidence
on the other hand
weigh a rival explanation
we may never know
accept an open ending

Which phrase fits?

1. The cup was still warm, so __________ someone had just left.
2. It's a striking photo, but __________ it is real.
3. The boats were still tied up, which __________ why they couldn't have left by sea.
4. No bone or letter was ever found, so __________ what happened.

Answer · mark your own

Which phrase fits?

1. …so it seems likely that someone had just left.
2. …but there's no proof that it is real.
3. …which the evidence suggests / that would explain why they couldn't have left by sea.
4. …so we may never know what happened.
