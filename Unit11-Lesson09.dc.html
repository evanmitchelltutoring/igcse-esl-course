Three beats, roughly a third each

Where, when, who. End it with locking the door yourself. ~50 words.
2 · Strange event
The open door + one detail that can't be explained. Slow it down. ~90 words.
3 · Reflection
How you felt, and what you make of it now. Leave a question. ~40 words.

Suspense toolkit · four moves

How to make a reader hold their breath

Slow the moment
Stretch the key beat: "I reached for the handle. It was already turning."
Hold detail back
Name the strange thing late. Let the reader guess first.
Appeal to the senses
A sound, a smell, the cold — "the hallway smelled of rain, indoors."
One short sentence
Land the shock in a short line. "The door was open."
A model · learn the shape, don't copy it

"The Door" — 3 beats

Last thing every night, I lock the back door and hang the key on its hook. On Saturday I did exactly that; I remember the cold click of it, and the rain starting against the glass as I climbed the stairs.

I woke because the house felt wrong. Downstairs, the hallway was colder than it should have been, and it smelled, faintly, of rain — indoors. I already knew before I turned the corner. The back door stood wide open, swinging a little. The key hung on its hook, exactly where I had left it. On the kitchen floor, a single wet footprint pointed inward, toward the stairs. By the time I found the torch, it had dried to nothing.

I still cannot explain it. The lock was not broken; the key had not moved. My aunt says old houses breathe. But houses do not leave footprints — and they do not, I think, come inside out of the rain.
