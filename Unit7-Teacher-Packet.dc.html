4 · Reading tasks

4a · "What Counts as a Sport?" — short answers
Answer key · 4a1 physical exertion, skill, organised competition, a recognised governing body.   2 organised competition — leagues, seasons, play-offs, referees.   3 the rapid, repeated hand movements and split-second reactions of elite players.   4 physical exertion.   5 whether exertion is the single decisive test, or one factor among four. Mark for precision; over-long answers with wrong extra detail can cancel.
4b · "The Business Behind the Team" — reading for detail
Answer key · 4b1 sponsorship (brands paying for logos on jerseys/streams).   2 streaming revenue (subscriptions/donations).   3 any two: head coach · analyst · team manager · sports psychologist (or academy staff).   4 any two: very short careers (retire mid-20s) · rosters change / dropped for poor form · modest, uncertain pay · burnout.
4c · "Bodies Pushed Hard" — note completion
Answer key · 4cDemands: reaction times · (deliberate) actions/movements.   Costs: repetitive strain · back and neck.   Responses: sports scientists · sleep (accept nutrition). Accept 1–3 words / close synonyms.
4d · "The Roaring Dark" — language & effect
Answer key · 4d1 metaphor/imagery — "a galaxy" makes the crowd vast, beautiful, awe-inspiring; spectators feel cosmic, not lonely.   2 simile — sound as a physical blow makes the atmosphere feel real and bodily/overwhelming.   3 repetition/list ("as one") — shows the crowd behaving exactly like a traditional sports crowd.   4 concedes the crowd doesn't prove gamers have an Olympic body, then reframes sport as spectacle — conceding makes the argument sound fair. Full credit needs device AND effect.

6 · Writing

6a · Directed report (Introduction → Findings → Recommendation)
Impersonal register: no "I think"; findings under headings; a reasoned recommendation to close. A non-defining relative clause + target words (criterion, legitimate, governing body, recognition).
6b · Argumentative essay (Stance → Concession → Reasoning → Own point → Verdict)
Concede then reason: grant the strongest objection, rebut with the four tests as evidence, land a verdict that resolves. Concession chunks + 4+ target words/chunks.

9b · "Watch for" — misconceptions specific to this unit
Second conditional (L7)would in the if-clause ("If I would train…"); will in the result; was for the unreal ("if I was captain"). Drill: if + past simple, would + base; prefer were for all persons.
Relative clauses (L7)Keeping the repeated word ("a player who he never gives up") and who/which mismatch. Drill: people → who, things → which/that; drop the repeat.
Short answers & note completion (L3, L5)Over-writing. Part 1 and note completion want the fewest correct words; extra wrong detail can cancel a right answer.
Language & effect (L6)Naming a device with no effect. Require the effect on the reader AND the word/image that creates it.
Report vs essay (L9 vs L10)Report drifting into "I think" / a letter; essay sitting on the fence. Report = impersonal, headed, recommendation; essay = clear stance, conceded counter, resolved verdict.
Debate (L11–L12)Flat contradiction ("you're wrong"). Require concede-then-rebut — "I take your point, but…" — backed by evidence from the readings.
9c · Support / Stretch
Support — give the frames: "If I were ___, I would ___." · "The findings indicate that ___." · "While critics argue ___, the evidence suggests ___." Word bank: criterion · exertion · sponsorship · reaction time · governing body · skill-based. Provide the report/essay skeleton pre-labelled so students only fill content.
Stretch — chain a concession and a second conditional in one essay sentence ("If exertion were the only test, darts would fail it too"); rebut the strongest opposing point, not the weakest; deliver the long turn from notes, not a script.
