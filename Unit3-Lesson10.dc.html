Warm-up · comparatives

Make the comparison in each gap

1. Water is ______ (effective) than an energy drink for concentration.
2. Walking is ______ (cheap) than a gym membership.
3. The ______ (regular) your sleep, the ______ (good) you feel.
4. Home-cooked meals are usually ______ (nutritious) than processed snacks.
Stuck? → short words add -er; longer words use more. "far/much" makes it stronger.
Push further → add a reason to Q1 with "because…".
Answer · mark your own

Make the comparison

1. Water is far more effective than an energy drink for concentration.
2. Walking is much cheaper than a gym membership.
3. The more regular your sleep, the better you feel. (the more… the better)
4. Home-cooked meals are usually more nutritious than processed snacks.

A model article

Read it · then write your own

Health Doesn't Have to Cost a Thing

Many students are convinced that being healthy means spending money they don't have. In reality this belief is misleading, and there is growing evidence that the costly option is often the very opposite of healthy.

Take energy drinks. They are expensive, yet water is far more effective for alertness, because the caffeine crash that follows can lead to tiredness and even mild dehydration. Short bursts of movement cost nothing too: a brisk walk wakes the brain and lifts the mood. Sleep matters most of all — the more regular your sleep, the better you focus.

The key is moderation, not money. In the long run, small daily habits beat any product you can buy. One simple step is to swap one energy drink for water, and notice the difference.

Does every idea have a reason?

One glow ✓
A strong title, a well-busted myth, or a nicely developed idea.
One grow →
An idea listed without a reason, a formal-slang slip, or a missing title.

Development — a reason for each idea — is what separates a mid-band article from a strong one.

Weak → strong · lift your writing

Say the same thing — better

✗ Weak
“Being healthy is not expensive. Drink water and sleep more.”
↓
✓ Strong
“Many students are convinced being healthy costs money — in reality that’s misleading: water is far more effective than an energy drink, and it costs nothing.”
Avoid these
  • Listing ideas with no reasons
  • Skipping the myth you are meant to bust
  • Slang / “hey guys” — keep it semi-formal
