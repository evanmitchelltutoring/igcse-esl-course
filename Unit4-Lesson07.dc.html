Teaching · copy this into your notes

have / has + past participle

I have travelled · she has seen · we have never eaten. Use it for experience (ever/never), recent news (just/already/yet), and time still going (for/since).
for vs since
for + a length: for two years.
since + a start point: since 2020.
where the adverb sits
ever/never/just/already go between have and the participle; yet goes at the end (negatives/questions).

Spot the error · answers

Corrected

1. I have visited three countries. (past participle)
2. She has lived in Tokyo for two years. (for + length)
3. Have you ever been to a festival abroad? (ever + participle)
4. He has already eaten the traditional food.
5. I haven't seen that ceremony yet. (yet → negative)
6. They have known each other since 2019.
