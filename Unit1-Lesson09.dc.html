Way In · what is a review?

A review gives an opinion — with reasons

DESCRIPTION — "The café has long desks and soft lamps."
REVIEW — "With its long desks and soft lamps, it's the calmest place to study in town — though it fills up fast."

A review always reaches a verdict, and helps the reader decide.

The shape of a review · five moves

Verdict → Describe → Highlight → Criticism → Recommend

1 · VerdictOpen with what the place is, and your overall opinion.
2 · DescribeBring the atmosphere alive — use unit words.
3 · HighlightThe one thing that makes it special.
4 · CriticismOne honest fault — this makes you believable.
5 · RecommendWho is it for? End with a recommendation.
A Grade-A review · study café
Verdict Describe Highlight Criticism Recommend

"The Long Table"

Tucked down a side street, The Long Table is the kind of café where nobody asks you to leave. You pay by the hour, not by the cup, so its warm, low-lit ambience invites you to linger over one drink for a whole afternoon. What makes it special is the quiet — it's a genuine sanctuary during exam season. My only criticism is that seats near the window fill fast, so arrive early. It's perfect for any student who needs somewhere calm to unwind and study. I'd recommend it to anyone.

BEFORE — "The café gets busy but I still like it."

Weak → strong · lift your writing

Say the same thing — better

✗ Weak

↓
✓ Strong
“Its warm, low-lit ambience makes you want to linger all afternoon.”
Avoid these
  • Only praise — no honest criticism
  • Vague words (nice, good)
  • No clear verdict at the end
