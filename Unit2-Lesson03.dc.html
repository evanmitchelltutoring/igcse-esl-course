The Plants That Break the Rules

Reading · extended

We tend to think of plants as quiet, patient things that stay where they are put. A few species, however, seem determined to break every rule we expect them to follow.

Take the welwitschia, which grows in the Namib Desert of southern Africa. It produces only two leaves in its entire life, yet it can live for more than a thousand years. The leaves never fall; instead they grow steadily, split and fray at the tips until the plant looks like a tangled heap of old ribbon. Botanists call it a living fossil, because it has barely changed in millions of years.

Far to the east, in the rainforests of Sumatra, the titan arum takes a different approach. Once every few years it unfurls a flower taller than an adult, which gives off a powerful smell of rotting meat. Visitors often hold their noses, but the stink is no accident: it draws in the flies and beetles that the plant depends on to carry its pollen. The bloom lasts barely two days before it collapses.

The resurrection plant is stranger still. During a drought it curls into a dry, brown ball that looks completely dead and can be blown across the ground by the wind. Yet add a little water and, within hours, it unrolls and turns green again, as though nothing had happened. Some specimens have been brought back to life after decades on a shelf.

Perhaps the boldest of all is the dancing plant of South Asia, whose small side-leaves twitch and rotate in response to warmth, light and even music. Nobody is quite sure why it moves; one theory is that the motion helps the plant find the brightest patches of sun.

What these plants share is a refusal to be ordinary. Each has found an unusual answer to the same question every living thing must face: how to survive.

Short answers · scan for the facts

Five questions

1. Which plant produces only two leaves in its whole life?
2. Why does the titan arum smell of rotting meat?
3. Which plant can come back to life after a drought?
4. What is unusual about how the dancing plant behaves?
5. What do all four plants have in common?
Stuck? → find the plant each question is about, then the detail.
Push further → answer using a relative clause (which / whose).
Support → Look in paragraph 2. Start: “It grows only ___ leaves, but lives for ___.”
Stretch → Why does the writer call it a “living fossil”?
Answers · mark your own

Check against the text

1. The welwitschia.
2. To attract the flies and beetles that carry its pollen.
3. The resurrection plant.
4. Its side-leaves twitch and rotate in response to warmth, light and music.
5. A refusal to be ordinary — each found an unusual way to survive.

which for things · whose for what it owns

Complete with which or whose
a) The titan arum, ____, smells of rotting meat.   (it smells)
b) The resurrection plant, ____ curls into a ball, survives drought.
c) The welwitschia, ____ two leaves never fall, is a living fossil.
Answers: a) which · b) which · c) whose
