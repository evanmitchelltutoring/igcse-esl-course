The idea · copy this into your notes

Three tests people apply to a sport

There's no single definition, but most gather three tests. Keep them handy — all unit we'll ask which ones a hard case (esports, darts, chess) passes, and which it fails.
Exertion
it demands real physical (or mental) effort
Skill
it is skill-based — ability decides, not luck
Competition
organised, with rules and a governing body

Six phrases for arguing a definition

it comes down to whether
name the real test that decides it
there is a strong case that
introduce a well-supported claim
the evidence suggests
base a claim on facts, not opinion
while critics argue
concede an opposing view before rebutting
weigh up
consider both sides before deciding
on balance
signal a weighed-up conclusion

Answer · mark your own

Which phrase fits?

1. Whether esports is a sport comes down to whether we count reflexes as physical effort.
2. The evidence suggests top gamers train as hard as athletes.
3. While critics argue that a screen game isn't "real" sport, the injuries are real enough.
4. On balance, I'd say it qualifies — the skill and competition are undeniable.
Watch me first · a model answer

My first view on the question

For me, it comes down to whether skill and competition are enough. While critics argue a sport must exhaust the whole body, on balance the evidence suggests esports meets most of the tests — so I'd say it counts.
