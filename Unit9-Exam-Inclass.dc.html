Insert — the text

Read the whole text once before you begin. Accounts are labelled A–D.

A — Hassan. I learned to swim at forty-five. As a child I had been afraid of water after a bad experience, and the fear followed me into adulthood. Booking that first lesson was the bravest thing I have ever done. My teacher was endlessly patient and never once made me feel foolish. Now I swim every week, and the fear is simply gone.

B — Greta. I taught myself to code using free videos online. I have a full-time job and two children, so I studied late at night when the house was quiet. Nobody pushed me; the discipline had to come entirely from me. It took two years, but last month I was offered a new job because of the skill — proof that the late nights were worth it.

C — Tomas. I started learning the violin at sixty, just for the joy of it. I will never play in an orchestra, and I do not care. People assume you learn an instrument to perform, but I do it purely for the pleasure of the sound. My grandchildren laugh at the squeaks, but they also clap when I get something right.

D — Priya. I went back to study for a university degree in my fifties, sitting in classes beside students younger than my own daughter. At first I felt I did not belong. But I soon realised that my years of life experience were an advantage, not an embarrassment — I understood the books in a way the younger students could not.

Section E — The writer's attitude (8 marks)
Plan first. Which words show a speaker's feeling, not just a fact? Jot two here before you write.
[2]E1. Hassan calls booking a lesson "the bravest thing I have ever done". What does this tell you about how he felt?

[4]E3. The title says "It's Never Too Late". Does the text support this? Explain, using two pieces of evidence from different accounts.
Marking — how your answers are judged
BandWhat it looks like
StrongAll factual answers correct and precise; summary in your own words, points only, within the word count. For attitude questions, you identify the feeling and support it with the exact words that carry it, explaining their effect.
SecureMost factual answers correct; summary covers the main points with some own wording. You show a feeling and point to some evidence, though the explanation of how the words work may be brief.
EAL note: You are marked on understanding, not perfect English. A correct idea in simple, clear words earns full marks. Use the class stems — "The speaker feels… because the word ___ suggests…" — to show your thinking.
