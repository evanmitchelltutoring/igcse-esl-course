Exam skill · before we listen

Listen for the advice and its reason

Experts give advice with a reason attached. A good listener catches both — the instruction, and the "because" that makes it stick.
Catch the instruction
"Stop moving." "Check the forecast."
Catch the reason
"…because walking in the dark gets you more lost."
Note the priority
"The single most important thing is…"

Track 1 · Lost in the woods · teacher reads twice

Listen, then answer

1. What is the single most important thing to do? ______
2. Why is walking on in the dark dangerous? ______
3. Why is water a bigger short-term concern than food? ______
4. Name one way to make yourself easy to spot. ______
Ear on the reasons → the speaker gives a "because" for each rule. Catch it.
Track 1 · answers

Listen, then answer

1. Stop moving.
2. It increases the risk of injury and leads you further from any path.
3. You can live weeks without food but only days without water.
4. Wear bright clothing or create a visible signal.

Track 2 · a mountain rescue interview · teacher reads twice

Listen, then answer

1. Who does Erin's team rescue most often — and why? ______
2. What is "the single most useful thing anyone can do"? ______
3. What is her advice to someone who is lost, and the reason? ______
4. How does Erin feel about being called a hero? ______
Ear on attitude → notice how Erin talks about the volunteers — is she boastful, or modest?
Track 2 · answers

Listen, then answer

1. Not the reckless but the unprepared — no map, no warm layer, wrong shoes.
2. Check the forecast before leaving — "and then actually believe it".
3. Stop and stay put, because walking on gets you more lost and more tired.
4. Modest — "we do not see it that way at all"; they just couldn't sit at home.
