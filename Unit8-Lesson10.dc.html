The shape · five beats

Position → Grant → Counter → Stand → Conclusion

1 · Position
Rephrase the issue and state your view clearly.
2 · Grant
Fairly acknowledge the strongest opposing point.
3 · Counter
Explain why it doesn't settle it — develop with a reason and example.
4 · Stand
Reinforce your view with a further reason and a prediction.
5 · Conclusion
Confirm your stand, looking forward.

Weak → strong · the concession move

Grant, then turn — don't drift

Weak (drifts)
"The money could build hospitals. Hospitals are very important and people need them. So maybe space is a waste."
Grants — then abandons its own side.
Strong (grants + turns)
"Admittedly, the opportunity cost is real: money on a telescope is money off a hospital. However, this ignores how much space research gives back — satellites that guide those very hospitals."
Grants fairly, then answers with a reason.

A model article · the opening three beats

"Reaching for the Stars, Forgetting the Ground?"

Every time a rocket roars into the sky, somebody asks the same question: should that money have stayed on Earth? Yet I believe space exploration is worth its cost, and that we must continue to fund it.

Let me be fair to the other side first. Critics point to the opportunity cost: every billion spent on a telescope is a billion not spent on hospitals. They argue governments should prioritise the suffering in front of them. This is a serious point, and I do not dismiss it.

However, the choice is not simply "space or Earth". Satellites born from space research now track storms and guide farmers; technologies built for astronauts have proved beneficial in medicine. Much of what we spend "up there" returns to us "down here".

Position — a clear view
Grant — the opposing point, fairly
Counter — "However…" + a reason
