Drop-in extra practice for the grammar lessons, matching the Units 1–2 template: each set has **Spot-the-Error (6)**, **Transform (6)**, a **Gap-Fill in context** (themed to the unit), and a **Build-Your-Own** (framed). Use as extra deck slides (Q then keyed A) or as a worksheet page in the packet. **Answer keys are TEACHER-ONLY.** Pitched for B1–B2 EAL learners. ## UNIT 3 · Health & Wellbeing — Cause–Effect & Advice (*because / so / therefore* · *should / ought to / had better*)

**A. Spot the error** (find and fix one mistake in each)
1. She feels calm because of she does yoga. → *She feels calm **because** she does yoga.* (*because* + clause; *because of* needs a noun)
2. I felt dizzy, so therefore I sat down. → *I felt dizzy, **so** I sat down.* (don't use *so* and *therefore* together)
3. You should to eat breakfast every day. → *You **should eat** breakfast every day.* (no *to* after *should*)
4. He ought exercise more often. → *He **ought to** exercise more often.* (*ought* takes *to*)
5. You had better not to skip meals. → *You **had better not** skip meals.* (no *to* after *had better*)
6. Because she felt unwell, so she stayed home. → *Because she felt unwell, she stayed home.* (don't pair *because* with *so*)

**B. Transform**
1. Join with *because*: "He was tired. He went to bed." → *He went to bed because he was tired.*
2. Join with *so*: "She felt stressed. She went for a walk." → *She felt stressed, so she went for a walk.*
3. Join with *therefore*: "Sugar harms your teeth. You should limit it." → *Sugar harms your teeth; therefore, you should limit it.*
4. Advice with *should*: (you / drink / more water) → *You should drink more water.*
5. Advice with *ought to*: (we / eat / less salt) → *We ought to eat less salt.*
6. Warning with *had better*: (you / not / miss / your medicine) → *You had better not miss your medicine.*

**C. Gap-fill** — cause–effect / advice
"Maria often feels tired __(1)__ she goes to bed very late. Her doctor says she __(2: modal)__ sleep eight hours a night. She eats a lot of fast food, __(3)__ she has little energy. 'You __(4)__ to eat more fruit and vegetables,' the doctor advised. 'And you __(5)__ not drink coffee after 6 p.m., __(6)__ it can keep you awake.'"
**Key:** 1 because · 2 should · 3 so · 4 ought · 5 had better · 6 because

**D. Build your own** (frames): *I feel ___ because ___ . / I was ___ , so ___ . / To stay healthy, you should ___ . / You had better not ___ .*

## UNIT 4 · Cultures & Connections — Present Perfect (*have/has* + past participle · *for/since/ever/never/just/already/yet*)

**A. Spot the error**
1. I have visit three countries. → *I have **visited** three countries.* (past participle)
2. She has lived in Tokyo since two years. → *She has lived in Tokyo **for** two years.* (*for* + a length of time)
3. Have you ever went to a festival abroad? → *Have you ever **been** to a festival abroad?* (*ever* + past participle)
4. He has already ate the traditional food. → *He has already **eaten** the traditional food.*
5. I have seen that ceremony yet. → *I **haven't** seen that ceremony yet.* (*yet* → negative)
6. They have knew each other since 2019. → *They have **known** each other since 2019.*

**B. Transform**
1. (I / just / finish / the book about India) → *I have just finished the book about India.*
2. (she / never / see / snow) → *She has never seen snow.*
3. (we / know / them / 2020) → *We have known them since 2020.*
4. (they / live / here / ten years) → *They have lived here for ten years.*
5. Question: (you / ever / eat / Korean food?) → *Have you ever eaten Korean food?*
6. (he / already / learn / three languages) → *He has already learned/learnt three languages.*

**C. Gap-fill** — present perfect
"My pen-friend Anya lives in Poland. We __(1: know)__ each other __(2)__ three years, but we __(3: never / meet)__ in person. She __(4: just / send)__ me photos of a festival. I __(5: already / learn)__ a few Polish words, but I __(6: not / visit)__ her country yet."
**Key:** 1 have known · 2 for · 3 have never met · 4 has just sent · 5 have already learned/learnt · 6 haven't visited (have not visited)

**D. Build your own** (frames): *I have never ___ . / I have ___ since ___ . / Have you ever ___ ? / I have just ___ .*

## UNIT 4 · Cultures & Connections — Defining Relative Clauses (*who / which / that / where*)

**A. Spot the error**
1. A festival is an event which it brings people together. → *A festival is an event **which/that** brings people together.* (drop the extra *it*)
2. That's the woman which taught me to dance. → *That's the woman **who/that** taught me to dance.* (people → *who/that*)
3. This is the city where I was born there. → *This is the city **where** I was born.* (drop *there*)
4. A market is a place who you can buy food. → *A market is a place **where** you can buy food.* (place → *where*)
5. Sushi is a dish who comes from Japan. → *Sushi is a dish **which/that** comes from Japan.* (thing → *which/that*)
6. This is the museum which I saw the mask. → *This is the museum **where** I saw the mask.* (place → *where*)

**B. Transform** (combine into one sentence with a relative clause)
1. "This is a tradition. It comes from my region." → *This is a tradition which/that comes from my region.*
2. "She is the guide. She showed us the temple." → *She is the guide who/that showed us the temple.*
3. "That is the village. My grandparents live there." → *That is the village where my grandparents live.*
4. "A translator is a person. This person changes one language into another." → *A translator is a person who/that changes one language into another.*
5. "Diwali is a festival. People celebrate it with lights." → *Diwali is a festival which/that people celebrate with lights.*
6. "This is the café. We met there." → *This is the café where we met.*

**C. Gap-fill** — relative pronouns (*who / which / that / where*)
"A souq is a market __(1)__ you can find spices and cloth. It is a place __(2)__ attracts many visitors. The trader __(3)__ sold me tea was very friendly. He is a man __(4)__ speaks four languages. I bought a lamp __(5)__ was made by hand, and I visited a mosque __(6)__ artists had painted the ceiling."
**Key:** 1 where · 2 which/that · 3 who/that · 4 who/that · 5 which/that · 6 where

**D. Build your own** (frames): *A ___ is a place where ___ . / I know someone who ___ . / This is the ___ that ___ . / I like food which ___ .*

## UNIT 5 · Art & AI — Modals of Speculation / Deduction (*must / might / could / can't* (+ *be / have been*))

**A. Spot the error**
1. This painting must to be very old. → *This painting **must be** very old.* (no *to* after *must*)
2. That can't been made by a computer. → *That **can't have been** made by a computer.* (*can't have* + past participle)
3. The artist might is famous. → *The artist **might be** famous.* (modal + base form *be*)
4. It musts be a photograph. → *It **must** be a photograph.* (modals take no *-s*)
5. She could to have painted it. → *She **could have** painted it.* (no *to* after *could*)
6. It must has been made by a computer. → *It **must have** been made by a computer.* (modal + *have* + past participle; modals never take *-s*)

**B. Transform** (rewrite using the modal in brackets)
1. "I'm sure this is AI art." (must) → *This must be AI art.*
2. "Perhaps the artist used a computer." (might) → *The artist might have used a computer.*
3. "It's impossible that a human painted this so fast." (can't) → *A human can't have painted this so fast.*
4. "Maybe it's a real photo." (could) → *It could be a real photo.*
5. "I'm certain she didn't draw this by hand." (can't) → *She can't have drawn this by hand.*
6. "Perhaps the machine made a mistake." (might) → *The machine might have made a mistake.*

**C. Gap-fill** — modals of deduction (use the cue in brackets)
"Look at this portrait. (I'm sure) the artist __(1)__ have great talent. (It's impossible that) a small child __(2)__ have painted it. (Maybe) a computer __(3)__ have helped — I'm not certain. (It's not possible that) it __(4)__ be an old painting, because the style is modern. (Perhaps) the museum __(5)__ show more works like this soon."
**Key:** 1 must · 2 can't · 3 might/could · 4 can't · 5 might/could

**D. Build your own** (frames): *This must be ___ . / It might/could be ___ . / A human can't have ___ . / The artist might have ___ .*

## UNIT 5 · Art & AI — Concession Linkers (*although / even though / despite / in spite of / however*)

**A. Spot the error**
1. Although the picture is fake, but it looks real. → *Although the picture is fake, it looks real.* (don't use *although* + *but*)
2. Despite it was made by AI, people liked it. → *Despite **being** made by AI, people liked it.* (*despite* + noun or *-ing*, not a clause)
3. However the app is free, many artists dislike it. → *Although the app is free, many artists dislike it.* (*however* is not a subordinator)
4. She is talented, despite she is young. → *She is talented **although/even though** she is young.* (*despite* cannot introduce a clause)
5. Despite of the criticism, he kept painting. → *Despite/In spite of the criticism, he kept painting.* (no *of* after *despite*)
6. Although it is expensive, however the software is popular. → *Although it is expensive, the software is popular.* (don't pair *although* with *however*)

**B. Transform** (join or rewrite using the linker in brackets)
1. "The image is fake. It looks real." (although) → *Although the image is fake, it looks real.*
2. "AI is fast. Many artists prefer to work by hand." (however) → *AI is fast; however, many artists prefer to work by hand.*
3. "The software is new. It is already popular." (despite) → *Despite being new, the software is already popular.*
4. "She had little training. She won the prize." (even though) → *Even though she had little training, she won the prize.*
5. "The picture was cheap. It became famous." (in spite of) → *In spite of being cheap, the picture became famous.*
6. "Robots can paint. They cannot feel emotion." (however) → *Robots can paint; however, they cannot feel emotion.*

**C. Gap-fill** — choose the linker (*although / even though / despite / in spite of / however*)
"__(1)__ AI can create art in seconds, many people still value human work. __(2)__ its speed, the technology cannot feel emotion. Some artists welcome the tools; __(3)__, others worry about their jobs. __(4)__ the software is cheap, galleries are careful about using it. The debate continues, __(5)__ the strong arguments on both sides."
**Key:** 1 Although/Even though · 2 Despite/In spite of · 3 however · 4 Although/Even though · 5 despite/in spite of

**D. Build your own** (frames): *Although ___ , ___ . / Despite ___ , ___ . / ___ ; however, ___ . / Even though ___ , ___ .*

**A. Spot the error**

5. She is wear a vintage jacket today. → *She is **wearing** a vintage jacket today.*

## UNIT 6 · Fashion & Identity — Reported Speech (*say/tell* · backshift · pronoun & time changes)

**A. Spot the error**
1. She said me that she liked my scarf. → *She **told** me / She **said** (that) she liked my scarf.* (*say* takes no indirect object without *to*; *tell* takes one)
2. He told that he was tired. → *He **said** that he was tired. / He **told me** that he was tired.* (*tell* needs an object)
3. She said she will buy the dress. → *She said she **would** buy the dress.* (backshift *will* → *would*)
4. He said he is happy with his new look. → *He said he **was** happy with his new look.* (backshift *is* → *was*)
5. She asked me what did I want. → *She asked me what I **wanted**.* (reported question: statement order, no auxiliary, backshift)
6. He said me to try the jacket. → *He **told** me to try the jacket.* (*tell* + object for commands)

**B. Transform** (direct → reported; backshift, pronoun & time changes)
1. "I like your shoes," she said. → *She said (that) she liked my shoes.*
2. "We are going to the show," they said. → *They said (that) they were going to the show.*
3. "I will wear black tomorrow," he said. → *He said (that) he would wear black the next day / the following day.*
4. "I bought this yesterday," she told me. → *She told me (that) she had bought that the day before / the previous day.*
5. "Do you like my hat?" he asked. → *He asked (me) whether/if I liked his hat.*
6. "Try the smaller size," the assistant told me. → *The assistant told me to try the smaller size.*

**C. Gap-fill** — complete the reported speech
"Yesterday my friend Leo showed me his new jacket. He said (that) he __(1)__ bought it at a vintage market. He told __(2)__ that the jacket __(3)__ over thirty years old. I asked him __(4)__ he had paid a lot, and he said it __(5)__ cheap. He also said he __(6)__ wear it to the party the next day."
**Key:** 1 had · 2 me · 3 was · 4 whether/if · 5 was · 6 would

**D. Build your own** (frames): *She said (that) she ___ . / He told me (that) ___ . / They said they would ___ . / She asked me whether ___ .*

## UNIT 7 · Sports & Competition — Second (Unreal) Conditional (*if* + past simple, *would* + base · + relative clauses)

**A. Spot the error**
1. If I would train harder, I would win. → *If I **trained** harder, I would win.* (no *would* in the *if*-clause)
2. If he practised more, he will score more goals. → *If he practised more, he **would** score more goals.* (*would* in the result)
3. If we had more players, we can win. → *If we had more players, we **could/would** win.*
4. I would be happy if I win a medal. → *I would be happy if I **won** a medal.* (unreal → past simple)
5. If they were fitter, they would played better. → *If they were fitter, they would **play** better.* (*would* + base form)
6. What would you do if you was the captain? → *What would you do if you **were** the captain?* (unreal *if I/he/she were*)

**B. Transform** (make a second conditional)
1. (I / be / taller → I / play / basketball) → *If I were taller, I would play basketball.*
2. (we / have / a coach → we / train / better) → *If we had a coach, we would train better.*
3. (she / not be / injured → she / run / the marathon) → *If she were not injured, she would run the marathon.*
4. (they / practise / more → they / win / more matches) → *If they practised more, they would win more matches.*
5. (you / be / the referee → what / you / do?) → *If you were the referee, what would you do?*
6. Add a relative clause: "A champion is a person. This person never gives up." → *A champion is a person who never gives up.*

**C. Gap-fill** — second conditional
"Our team is good, but we do not always win. If we __(1: have)__ a better coach, we __(2: train)__ more effectively. If our striker __(3: be)__ fitter, he __(4: score)__ more goals. Honestly, if I __(5: be)__ the captain, I __(6: change)__ our tactics. A player who never gives up is often the one who wins."
**Key:** 1 had · 2 would train · 3 were/was · 4 would score · 5 were/was · 6 would change

**D. Build your own** (frames): *If I were ___ , I would ___ . / If our team had ___ , we would ___ . / What would you do if ___ ? / A great athlete is someone who ___ .*

## UNIT 8 · Space & Ambition — Future Forms for Predicting / Prioritising (*will / going to / might* · *first…then…finally*)

**A. Spot the error**
1. I think humans will to live on Mars. → *I think humans **will live** on Mars.* (no *to* after *will*)
2. She is going visit the space centre. → *She is going **to visit** the space centre.*
3. They will probably lands on the Moon again. → *They will probably **land** on the Moon again.* (*will* + base form)
4. The mission will maybe succeed. → *The mission **might** succeed.* (*will maybe* is non-standard; use *might* for possibility)
5. We will explore Mars, then first we will build a base. → *First we will build a base, then we will explore Mars.* (sequence order)
6. He is going to studies astronomy at university. → *He is going to **study** astronomy at university.*

**B. Transform** (use the future form in brackets)
1. Prediction: (humans / reach / Mars) (will) → *Humans will reach Mars.*
2. Plan: (she / study / physics) (going to) → *She is going to study physics.*
3. Possibility: (we / find / life in space) (might) → *We might find life in space.*
4. Evidence-based prediction: "Look at those clouds — (it / rain / on launch day)" (going to) → *It is going to rain on launch day.*
5. Order the steps: train → launch → explore (first…then…finally) → *First we will train, then we will launch, and finally we will explore.*
6. Possibility: (the mission / be / delayed) (might) → *The mission might be delayed.*

**C. Gap-fill** — future forms (mind the cues)
"My dream is to become an astronaut. Next year I __(1: going to)__ study science at university — I have already chosen the course. After that, I __(2: might)__ apply to the space agency, though I'm not certain. I believe that one day humans __(3: will)__ live on Mars. Our plan is clear: __(4)__ we will train hard, __(5)__ we will pass the tests, and __(6)__ we will fly into space."
**Key:** 1 am going to · 2 might · 3 will · 4 first · 5 then · 6 finally

**D. Build your own** (frames): *In the future, I think ___ will ___ . / Next year I am going to ___ . / We might ___ . / First ___ , then ___ , and finally ___ .*

## UNIT 9 · Learning & Growth — Gerunds vs Infinitives (*enjoy doing · decide to do · prefer …ing · want to …*)

**A. Spot the error**
1. I enjoy to learn new languages. → *I enjoy **learning** new languages.* (*enjoy* + *-ing*)
2. She decided studying medicine. → *She decided **to study** medicine.* (*decide* + *to*)
3. We want improving our English. → *We want **to improve** our English.* (*want* + *to*)
4. He avoided to make mistakes. → *He avoided **making** mistakes.* (*avoid* + *-ing*)
5. They hope finishing the course soon. → *They hope **to finish** the course soon.* (*hope* + *to*)
6. I finished to write my essay last night. → *I finished **writing** my essay last night.* (*finish* + *-ing*)

**B. Transform** (complete with a gerund or an infinitive)

5. (they / plan / study / abroad) → *They plan to study abroad.*
6. (I / prefer / work / in the morning) → *I prefer working in the morning. / I prefer to work in the morning.* (both correct)

I want __(4: pass)__ my exams, so I practise __(5: write)__ essays at the weekend. I prefer __(6: study)__ in the library, because it is quiet."
**Key:** 1 to improve · 2 reading · 3 making / to make (both correct) · 4 to pass · 5 writing · 6 studying / to study (both correct)

## UNIT 10 · A Changing World — Hedging + Subordination (*may / might / tend to / seem to / it appears* · *although / while / whereas*)

**A. Spot the error**
1. Sea levels may to rise this century. → *Sea levels **may rise** this century.* (no *to* after *may*)
2. People tend to wasting food. → *People tend **to waste** food.* (*tend to* + base form)
3. Rich countries pollute a lot, while poor countries pollutes little. → *…while poor countries **pollute** little.* (subject–verb agreement)
4. Although scientists warn us, but people ignore them. → *Although scientists warn us, people ignore them.* (don't use *although* + *but*)
5. The temperature seem to be rising. → *The temperature **seems** to be rising.* (subject–verb agreement)
6. Renewable energy might solves the problem. → *Renewable energy might **solve** the problem.* (*might* + base form)

**B. Transform** (rewrite with the hedging or linking word in brackets)
1. "Sea levels are rising." (make it tentative — may) → *Sea levels may be rising.*
2. "People waste a lot of water." (tend to) → *People tend to waste a lot of water.*
3. "The situation is serious." (it appears that) → *It appears that the situation is serious.*
4. "Cars cause pollution. Bicycles are clean." (whereas) → *Cars cause pollution, whereas bicycles are clean.*
5. "The plan is expensive. It could save the planet." (although) → *Although the plan is expensive, it could save the planet.*
6. "Cities are getting hotter." (seem to) → *Cities seem to be getting hotter.*

**C. Gap-fill** — hedging + subordination
"Climate change __(1: may)__ be the biggest challenge of our time. Scientists say that temperatures __(2: tend)__ to rise each decade, and it __(3: appear)__ that extreme weather is becoming more common. Wealthy nations produce most of the pollution, __(4)__ poorer nations suffer the most. __(5)__ governments have promised to act, progress is slow. Renewable energy __(6: seem)__ to offer hope for the future."
**Key:** 1 may · 2 tend · 3 appears · 4 whereas/while · 5 Although/While · 6 seems

**D. Build your own** (frames): *___ may ___ . / People tend to ___ . / It appears that ___ . / ___ , whereas ___ .*

## UNIT 11 · Mystery & the Unexplained — Narrative Past Tenses + Past Deduction (past simple/continuous/perfect · *must have / might have / can't have* + past participle)

**A. Spot the error**
1. I was reading when suddenly the lights go out. → *I was reading when suddenly the lights **went** out.* (past simple for the sudden action)
2. While we were walk in the forest, we heard a noise. → *While we were **walking** in the forest, we heard a noise.*
3. The house was empty because someone had leave. → *The house was empty because someone had **left**.* (past perfect + past participle)
4. It must been a ghost. → *It **must have been** a ghost.* (*must have* + past participle)
5. He can't have saw the creature; it was too dark. → *He can't have **seen** the creature; it was too dark.* (*can't have* + past participle)
6. They might left before midnight. → *They might **have left** before midnight.* (*might have* + past participle)

**B. Transform**
1. Past continuous + past simple: (I / sleep / when / the phone / ring) → *I was sleeping when the phone rang.*
2. Past perfect: (when we arrived, the visitors / already / leave) → *When we arrived, the visitors had already left.*
3. Deduction: "I'm sure it was the wind." (must have) → *It must have been the wind.*
4. Deduction: "Perhaps a fox made the sound." (might have) → *A fox might have made the sound.*
5. Deduction: "It's impossible that he heard us." (can't have) → *He can't have heard us.*
6. Narrative: (the door / open / while / she / read) → *The door opened while she was reading.*

**C. Gap-fill** — narrative past + past deduction

## UNIT 12 · Adventure & Survival — Conditionals (first + second) + Reported Speech

**A. Spot the error**
1. If you will get lost, use a map. → *If you **get** lost, use a map.* (first conditional: no *will* in the *if*-clause)
2. If we don't hurry, we will missed the boat. → *If we don't hurry, we **will miss** the boat.* (*will* + base form)
3. If I were a guide, I will take the safe path. → *If I were a guide, I **would** take the safe path.* (second conditional result: *would*)
4. She said she will help us if we needed it. → *She said she **would** help us if we needed it.* (reported speech backshift)
5. The guide told to us to stay together. → *The guide **told us** to stay together.* (*tell* + object, no *to*)
6. If it rains tomorrow, we cancelled the hike. → *If it rains tomorrow, we **will cancel** the hike.* (first conditional result)

**B. Transform**
1. First conditional: (if / you / touch / that plant → your skin / burn) → *If you touch that plant, your skin will burn.*
2. First conditional: (if / we / not find / water → we / be / in danger) → *If we do not find water, we will be in danger.*
3. Second conditional: (if / I / have / a boat → I / cross / the river) → *If I had a boat, I would cross the river.*
4. Second conditional: (if / they / be / more careful → they / not get / lost) → *If they were more careful, they would not get lost.*
5. Reported speech: "I will lead the group," the guide said. → *The guide said (that) she/he would lead the group.*
6. Reported command: "Don't drink that water," she told us. → *She told us not to drink that water.*

**C. Gap-fill** — conditionals + reported speech
"Before the trek, the guide gave us some advice. She said that if we __(1: get)__ lost, we __(2)__ stay calm and wait. 'If you __(3: follow)__ the river, you will reach the village,' she explained. She also told us __(4)__ to leave the group alone. I thought to myself: if I __(5: be)__ braver, I __(6: explore)__ the caves — but that would be dangerous."
**Key:** 1 got · 2 should · 3 follow · 4 not · 5 were/was · 6 would explore
