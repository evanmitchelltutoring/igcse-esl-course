The River That Came Back to Life

Reading · notes

Thirty years ago the River Mell, which runs through the small town of Casterton, was little more than an open drain. Factories upstream had poured waste into it for decades, the water ran a strange grey-green, and the fish had long since disappeared. Most residents simply kept their backs to it.

The change began with a single retired teacher, Pamela Osei, who started picking litter from the banks on her morning walks. Neighbours who had once ignored the river began to join her, and within a year a small volunteer group had formed. They called themselves the River Watch.

Their first victory was information. By testing the water themselves and sharing the results online, they put quiet pressure on the factories until two of the worst polluters agreed to install filters. Cleaner water alone, however, was not enough. The group also planted reeds along the banks, which trap dirt and give insects and birds somewhere to shelter, and they removed several old concrete walls so that the river could spread into its natural floodplain after heavy rain.

Slowly, life returned. Dragonflies appeared first, then small fish, and three years ago an otter — an animal not seen in Casterton for half a century — was photographed at dawn. The town now holds a River Festival every spring, and local schools use the banks as an outdoor classroom.

Pamela is modest about her part. 'I never set out to start a movement,' she says. 'I just couldn't walk past the mess any more. The lesson, if there is one, is that a place only stays neglected for as long as nobody decides to care for it.'

Skill · completing notes

Three rules for note-completion

1 · Count your words
One to four words only. A full sentence is too long.
2 · Use the text's words
Lift the exact word or phrase where you can; spell it correctly.
3 · Follow the order
The gaps come in the same order as the text — so you can work top to bottom.

Grammar · telling what happened

The past simple — a finished sequence

Regular: +-ed · "poured", "planted", "started"
Irregular: "began", "ran", "gave", "was / were"
Put the verb in the past simple
a) The factories ____ (pour) waste into the river.
b) A retired teacher ____ (begin) picking up litter.
c) The group ____ (plant) reeds along the banks.
d) An otter ____ (be) photographed at dawn.
Answers: a) poured · b) began · c) planted · d) was
