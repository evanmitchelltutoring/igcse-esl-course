4 · Reading tasks

4a · "How a Trend Travels" — short answers
Answer key · 4a1 a designer's runway show, or a teenager's short video of an outfit.   2 an influencer with millions of followers picks it up (it goes viral).   3 about a year.   4 it falls / fades — the people who started it have moved on.   5 "Where did this come from?" and "How long before it disappears?" Mark for precision; over-long answers with wrong extra detail can cancel.
4b · "Why I Stopped Dressing for Other People" — feeling & inference
Answer key · 4b1 being noticed felt dangerous — "if no one looked at me, no one could judge me."   2 invisible (the word ending paragraph 1, "completely invisible").   3 for the first time her clothes "said something, and someone listened" — after years of having no style.   4 confidence came the other way around — choosing true clothes made her more confident, not the reverse. Full credit needs the feeling named AND the word/effect.
4c · "The Real Price of Cheap Clothes" — summary
Answer key · 4cProblems (any 2): worker exploitation — very low pay, long hours, unsafe factories; heavy environmental cost — water, energy, dye pollution, mountains of waste.   Solutions (any 2): buy fewer, better-made items; choose second-hand; support brands that pay fairly and produce less.   Conceded: fast fashion makes style affordable and provides jobs.   Summary (≈60 words): reward own-words points only, within count; penalise lifted sentences and over-length.
4d · "The Grey Army" — language & effect
Answer key · 4d1 metaphor — students lose individuality, become one faceless mass; anonymous, faintly unsettling.   2 two images = two sides: a "curtain" protects/hides (kindness); a "lid" presses down/traps (suppression).   3 the flames = small acts of individuality / self-expression flickering against the sameness.   4 repetition hammers the flat dullness, so the "colour leaking through" stands out. Full credit needs the device/image AND its effect.

6 · Writing — the Style Profile

6a · The Style Interview (Introduce → Report → Round off)
Report, don't transcript: reported speech with backshift (she told me… he explained…), person set up first, insight to close. Reported speech + 4 target words.
6b · Describe Someone's Style (Scene → Detail → Verdict)
Show, don't tell: concrete detail + expanded noun phrases (opinion before fact), present continuous held; end on what the style reveals.

9b · "Watch for" — misconceptions specific to this unit
Present continuous (L7)Dropping is/are ("She wearing"), leaving the base verb ("is change"), or subject-verb mismatch ("more people is"). Also the word order of more and more. Drill: subject + is/are + verb-ing; more and more before the noun/adjective.
Reported speech (L8)said me for told me; no backshift ("she said she loves it"); question word order kept ("what did I want"). Drill: tell + person / say without; back one tense; reported questions use statement order + if/whether.
Reading for feeling (L4)Stopping at "it's sad". Require name-it-then-prove-it: the feeling AND the word/image that carries it.
Summary (L5)Copying whole sentences and running over length. Require own words, points only (no examples/repetition), strict word count.
Language & effect (L6)Naming a device with no effect. Require the effect on the reader AND the word/image that creates it.
Interview vs description (L10 vs L11)L10 must report, not print a Q-and-A; L11 must show with detail, not tell ("she is stylish") — and must end on a verdict, not a list of clothes.
9c · Support / Stretch
Support — give the frames: "More and more people are ___." · "She told me (that) she ___." · "The first thing you notice about ___ is ___." Word bank: trend · influencer · self-expression · conform · fast fashion · thrift · signature. Provide the interview/description skeleton pre-labelled so students only fill content.
Stretch — combine present continuous and reported speech in the interview ("She told me she was buying less"); replace every "telling" adjective with a "shown" detail; deliver a 60-second spoken version of the description.

EAL note: reward communication that succeeds despite minor slips; do not over-penalise L1-influenced error where meaning is clear. Interview 120–200 words; description 120–160 words.
