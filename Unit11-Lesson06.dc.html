Exam skill · before we read

A reliable witness marks the edges of what they know

Trust grows, oddly, when a witness admits what they didn't see. Watch for hedged language and honest gaps — and separate what was seen from what was assumed.
Hedged language
"I'm fairly sure…", "perhaps", "I cannot be certain".
Admitted gaps
"I never saw their faces." Naming a limit builds trust.
Saw vs assumed
"Two people got out" (saw) vs "they were robbers" (assumed).

An Eyewitness Account

Underline doubt & certainty

I have been asked to write down exactly what I saw on the night of the fourteenth, while it is still fresh in my memory.

It was just after eleven o'clock. I could not sleep, so I was standing at my bedroom window, which looks out over the square. The street was empty and very quiet, and a light rain was falling.

That was when I noticed the van. It was parked outside the jeweller's shop on the far corner, with its lights off, which struck me as odd at that hour. I am fairly sure it was dark blue, though the street lamp nearest it was broken, so I cannot be completely certain about the colour.

After a minute or two, two people got out. Both wore dark coats with the hoods up, so I could not see their faces at all. One of them was noticeably taller than the other. The shorter one carried a bag, quite large and square. They walked quickly to the shop door and stood there with their backs to me for perhaps thirty seconds. I did not hear any glass break.

Then a car came into the square, and its headlights swept across the corner. The moment the light touched them, both figures turned and walked away, unhurried, in opposite directions. The van stayed where it was. When I looked again at about half past eleven, it had gone.

I want to be honest about what I am not sure of. I never saw either person's face. I cannot swear to the colour of the van. And I cannot say for certain that any crime took place at all — only that what I saw seemed strange enough to report.

Answer · mark your own

Saw it, or assumed it?

1. S — plainly seen and stated.
2. A (uncertain) — "fairly sure… cannot be completely certain".
3. S — "noticeably taller".
4. A — she refuses it: "cannot say for certain any crime took place".
5. S — directly observed.

Her honesty about the gaps is exactly what makes her a reliable witness.
