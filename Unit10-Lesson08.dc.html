Exam skill · the listening routine

Read → predict → listen → check

You hear each item twice. Use the first play to find the answer and the second to confirm it. Beware the word-match trap.
For gist
Don't chase every word. Listen for the overall message — what would the speaker say if you asked "so, what's your point?"
For matching
A speaker may say "family" without matching a statement about family. Match the whole meaning, not the word.

Exercise 1 · Part 1 · a student assembly talk

Listen for the gist — answer in a few words

1. What one problem did the year group decide to fix? ______
2. Name one change they made. ______
3. By how much did food waste fall? ______
4. What is the speaker's main point? ______
Support → Q4 is the gist — it's not a fact, it's why the project mattered.
Exercise 1 · answers

Listen for the gist

1. Food waste at lunch.
2. Any one: changed the ordering · smaller portions · a sharing shelf.
3. By nearly half.
4. Doing something feels different from doing nothing — and makes the next thing possible.
