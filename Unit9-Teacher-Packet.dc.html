4 · Reading tasks

4a · "Teaching Myself, One Tab at a Time" — short answers
Answer key · 4a1 No animation course at school; the studio was two hours away.   2 Deciding which videos were worth her time.   3 What went wrong and what she would try next.   4 Four seconds.   5 You can practise mistakes until they feel normal — no one corrects you. Mark for precision; over-long answers with wrong extra detail can cancel.
4b · "The People Who Helped Me Learn" — multiple matching
Answer key · 4b1 A (Tomas) — explaining to others taught him most.   2 D (Sam) — the group would notice if he disappeared.   3 C (Lena) — equals, so mistakes felt safe.   4 A (Tomas) — valued the speed of an answer. Note A and C are both online — match the meaning, not the shared word.
4c · "The Most Useful Thing You'll Get Wrong" — note completion
Answer key · 4cA hidden mistake is one you cannot learn from.   Step 1 analyse · Step 2 invite (constructive) feedback · Step 3 make a (deliberate) plan.   It takes determination / resilience to look at your own errors. Accept 1–3 words / close synonyms; over the limit loses the mark.
4d · "You Will Never Finish Learning" — choose & effect
Answer key · 4d1 (b) learning never ends, and that is an opportunity.   2 Refusing to learn is now the real risk ("standing still" is a metaphor, not literal).   3 "dustbin of the obsolete" makes lost skills sound like rubbish/worthless, so change feels urgent.   4 any optimistic line, e.g. "you are never too late, never finished, never trapped" or "an open door". Full credit for effect needs the word/image AND its effect.

6 · Writing

6a · Informal email (Greeting → the spot → how it helped → invite)
Friendly register: greeting, contractions, warm sign-off; all three content points, and the invite must name a when. A gerund + a present perfect continuous. 120–160 words. Full model ("A study spot I found") in L10.
6b · Argumentative essay (Position → 2× P-R-R-E → Conclusion)
Develop, don't list: each body paragraph is a full Point → Reason → Result → Example; one position held throughout; a forward-looking conclusion. A gerund + a present perfect continuous; target phrases. 120–160 words. Full model in L11.

9b · "Watch for" — misconceptions specific to this unit
Gerunds vs infinitives (L7)"want learning", "enjoy to learn", and -ing dropped after a preposition ("good at learn"). Drill: check the word before — verb from the -ing list / the to list, or a preposition (always -ing, incl. "look forward to learning").
Present perfect continuous (L8)Dropping been ("I have studying"); confusing for (a length) and since (a start); using it with state verbs ("been knowing"). Drill: have/has + been + -ing; for/since; state verbs → perfect simple.
Short answers & note completion (L3, L4)Over-writing. Part 1 and note completion want the fewest correct words; extra wrong detail can cancel, and over the word limit scores zero.
Multiple matching (L5)Word-match errors — picking a person because they share a word with the statement. Drill: put the statement in your own words, then match the whole meaning.
Part 4 choose & effect (L6)Choosing a half-true option, or naming an image with no effect. Require ruling out near-misses, and quoting the word AND its effect.
Email vs essay (L10 vs L11)Email drifting into a formal letter; essay listing points with no development. Email = warm, all points, an invite with a when; essay = one position, each reason a full P-R-R-E.
Debate (L12)Flat contradiction. Require concede-then-answer — "I take your point that…, but…" — backed by a reason.
9c · Support / Stretch
Support — give the frames: "I can't ___ yet, but I've been ___." · "Firstly, ___, because ___. As a result, ___. For example, ___." · "I take your point that ___, but ___." Word bank: self-directed · obsolete · feedback · perseverance · mentor · adapt. Provide the email/essay skeleton pre-labelled so students only fill content.
Stretch — chain a gerund and a present perfect continuous in one sentence ("I enjoy learning languages and I've been studying Spanish since September"); develop each P-R-R-E with a real, named example; in the debate, concede the strongest objection, not the weakest.
