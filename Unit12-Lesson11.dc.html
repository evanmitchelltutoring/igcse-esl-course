talk
Part 1 · interview warm-up

Answer naturally — and give a reason

Do you enjoy outdoor activities like walking, climbing or camping? Why, or why not?
Would you say you are a cautious person, or someone who takes risks?
Some people love the idea of an expedition to a wild place; others find it frightening. Which are you?
Do you think young people today spend enough time outdoors? Why?
Golden rule → never a one-word answer. Add "…because…" every time.

The frame · develop every point

P → R → R → E

Point
say what happened
Reason
because…
Result
so…
Example
for example…
"The scariest moment was losing the path (Point), because the mist came down so fast (Reason), so we had to stop and wait (Result) — we sheltered in a hollow for the whole night, for example (Example)."
Part 2 · the long turn

Talk about an adventure, or a time you faced danger

You could talk about…
  • an adventure you had, or a time you faced danger or difficulty
  • where you were, who you were with, and what happened
  • how you felt during it, and what you did
  • what you learned from the experience
  • the idea that facing difficulty is good for young people
Support → use one card point per sentence, each built out with P-R-R-E.
Stretch → weave in keep a clear head, composure, and an "If I had…" line.
talk
Part 3 · the discussion

Weigh it out loud — give a reason each time

1. Why do you think some people are drawn to dangerous activities and adventures?
2. What is the most important thing to do if you find yourself in a dangerous situation?
3. Is it the responsibility of schools to give students more challenging, outdoor experiences?
Justify + extend: "I'd say… because… — though you could argue…"
