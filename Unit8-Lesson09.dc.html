Decode the task · before you write

The task, in plain words

Your school website reviews films, exhibitions and events. Write a review (150–200 words) of a space exhibition you visited or a space documentary you watched.
Describe
What was it? Where and when did you see it?
Judge
One high point and one weaker point — and why.
Recommend
Would you recommend it? Who would enjoy it most?

The shape · three beats

Scene → Detail → Verdict

Scene
Hook the reader, then say what it was, where and when — in one or two lines. Not "I am going to review…".
Detail
Specific things you saw and your reaction — one high point, one weaker. A light future form fits: "visitors will love…".
Verdict
A clear recommendation, and who would enjoy it most. Don't trail off — leave a decision.
show me
Words to use · at least three

Evaluative adjectives that judge

immersive
surrounds you completely — "an immersive opening"
breathtaking
so beautiful it stops you — "a breathtaking dome"
hands-on
you touch and try things — "a hands-on lab"
cramped · underwhelming
for the weak point — "cramped and underwhelming"
show me
Weak → strong · make it vivid

Describe, then judge — don't just rate

Weak
"The exhibition was good. There were lots of things to see. I give it 8 out of 10."
Vague, no specifics, no real judgement.
Strong
"The hands-on lunar lab was the highlight — you can lift a 'moon rock' and steer a rover. Younger visitors will love it; the final room, though, felt cramped and underwhelming."
Specifics + evaluative words + a future form.
A model review · watch the three beats

"Walking on the Moon" — a review

Last weekend I visited "Walking on the Moon", a new exhibition at the City Science Centre, and I am still thinking about it.

The moment you step inside, the lights dim and a vast curved screen surrounds you with footage of Earth from orbit — a genuinely immersive opening. My favourite section was the hands-on lunar lab, where you can lift a "moon rock" and steer a model rover. Watching the planets drift overhead was simply breathtaking.

Not everything worked, however. The final room was cramped and badly signposted, so the panels were hard to read. After such a start, it felt underwhelming.

Even so, I would strongly recommend it. Anyone curious about science will love it, and younger visitors especially will leave inspired. Book early — it is going to be popular.
