Insert — the text

Read the whole text once before you begin. Paragraphs are numbered.

1  As summers grow hotter, cities face a problem that the countryside does not. Concrete, brick and tarmac soak up the sun's heat all day and release it slowly at night, so a city can stay several degrees warmer than the fields around it. Scientists call this the "urban heat island" effect. For the people living there, it means restless, sweaty nights and rising electricity bills as air conditioners run without stopping.

2  The city of Medellín, in Colombia, decided to fight back not with machines but with plants. Engineers and gardeners worked together to create what they called "green corridors": long ribbons of trees and climbing plants planted along thirty of the city's roads and waterways. The leaves provide shade, and as water evaporates from them the surrounding air is cooled, much as sweat cools the skin.

3  The results were measured carefully. Within a few years, the average temperature along the corridors had fallen by about two degrees. On the hottest streets, the drop was even greater. The shaded paths also tempted more people to walk or cycle instead of driving, which in turn lowered the amount of pollution in the air.

4  There were other benefits the planners had not fully expected. The new plants gave homes to birds and insects, bringing wildlife back into the heart of the city. Residents reported that the green streets simply felt calmer and more pleasant. Best of all, once the plants were established, the corridors cost very little to maintain — far less than the energy needed to cool the city by machine.

5  Cities around the world are now studying Medellín's example. The lesson is a hopeful one: sometimes the cheapest and oldest technology — a tree — is also the best.

Marking — how your answers are judged
BandWhat it looks like
StrongAll factual answers correct and precise; summary in your own words, points only, within the word count. For attitude questions, you identify the writer's opinion and support it with the exact words that carry it, explaining their effect.
SecureMost factual answers correct; summary covers the main points with some own wording. You show the writer has a view and point to some evidence, though the explanation of how the words work may be brief.
EAL note: You are marked on understanding, not perfect English. A correct idea in simple, clear words earns full marks. Use the class stems — "The writer feels… because the word ___ suggests…" — to show your thinking.
