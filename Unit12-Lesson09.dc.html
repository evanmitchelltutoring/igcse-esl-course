Your task · read it carefully

An email to a friend

Something dramatic happened to you recently on a trip — you got lost, were caught out by the weather, or had a narrow escape. A friend has heard a rumour and wants to know what really happened. Write an email of 150–200 words recounting it.
1 · explain where you were and what went wrong
2 · describe what happened and how you felt
3 · say how it ended and what you learned
Today you will…

Three things by the end of the lesson

1Write in a warm, informal voice that sounds like a real email.
2Recount events in order, with controlled narrative tenses.
3End with feeling and a lesson — one "If I had…" line.
Why it matters: a recount email rewards a genuine voice and a clear sequence — not the most dramatic plot.

Voice toolkit · four moves

How to sound like a real friend writing

Talk to them
"You won't believe this…", "Guess what happened!"
Short, punchy sentences
Drop one in at the scary moment. "Then the mist came down."
Name the feeling
"I was terrified", "my heart was pounding", "I nearly gave up".
Warm sign-off
"Write back soon!", "Miss you", "Love, ___".
A model · learn the shape, don't copy it

"Hi Sam…"

Hi Sam, you will not believe what happened to me last weekend! You probably heard I got into trouble up on the hills — well, it's true, and it was the most frightening hour of my life.

I'd gone walking on my own, which was my first mistake. The path was clear at the start, but after the river the mist came down and I lost the trail completely. Suddenly I was stranded: the light was fading, my water had run out, and for a few minutes I lost all my composure — my heart pounding, no idea which way to turn. Then I remembered what my uncle always says: find water and follow it downhill. It took all my willpower to keep going on the treacherous rocks, until the stream finally led me to a farmhouse, where the owners gave me shelter.

If I had panicked, I dread to think how it might have ended. I've learned my lesson, though — next time I'll check the forecast, and never go up there alone. Write back soon! Love, Alex
