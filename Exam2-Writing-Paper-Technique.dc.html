Clinic 1 · Directed writing

Cover every content point

The trap
Writing fluently about two bullets and quietly forgetting the third. A missed content point caps the whole content mark, however good the prose.
The fix · one bullet, one paragraph
Number the bullets. Make each its own paragraph, and develop it with a reason or a consequence. Tick each bullet off before you finish.
"Develop" means one idea + a reason or result — not two ideas half-stated. Depth beats breadth.
Rep · one developed sentence per bullet

Task: article on extending the library's hours

Some students need quiet space to study after school; others say the library already closes too early for anyone with after-school clubs. Write an article giving your views.
Bullet 1 · why extended hours would help → ______ + a reason
Bullet 2 · answer the clubs-finish-late worry → ______ + a response
Bullet 3 · recommend a trial → ______ + why now
Check: three bullets → three paragraphs, each with a reason. The strongest answers respond to bullet 2 rather than just mentioning it.
Clinic 2 · Register & tone

Match the voice to the reader

The trap
Writing every task in the same voice — slang in a formal report, or a stiff "I am writing to inform you" in an email to a close friend. Register alone can cap the mark.
The fix · name the reader first
Before writing, say who reads this. Friend → contractions, warmth, "Hi". Editor/council → formal, no contractions, "Dear…". Let the reader set greeting, vocabulary and sign-off.
Consistency matters most: pick the register on line one and hold it to the sign-off.
Rep · formal or informal?

Label each — and name where it belongs

1. "I am writing in response to your recent article…" F / I?
2. "Guess what — I finally joined the photography club!" F / I?
3. "It is recommended that the council approve this scheme." F / I?
4. "Honestly I was really disappointed too." F / I?
Answers: 1 F (letter to editor) · 2 I (email to sibling) · 3 F (report to council) · 4 I (email to friend). A report that slips into "Honestly…" or an email that opens "I am writing to inform you" loses the register mark.
Clinic 3 · The 5-minute plan

Five minutes now saves the whole answer

The trap
Starting to write immediately, then stalling at paragraph two with no plan — or repeating the same idea in different words to fill the space.
The fix · a 5-minute skeleton
Jot the three bullets, one idea + one development beside each, and number the order. Five minutes of planning buys a structured, non-repetitive answer.
A plan is also insurance: if you run short of time, a planned skeleton still reads as a complete, ordered answer.
Rep · build a 5-minute plan

Task: report recommending a school recycling scheme

Most students throw away paper and plastic mixed together; a nearby school's scheme cut its waste bill. Summarise the problem, outline a scheme, recommend it.
Findings · problem + the survey detail → ______
Proposed scheme · three ordered steps → ______
Recommendation · approve + the reason → ______
Model plan: Findings (single bins → nothing recyclable; nearby school cut costs) → Scheme (1 labelled bins · 2 assembly · 3 termly check) → Recommend (approve next term; low-risk, clear benefit).
Clinic 4 · Mark-loss patterns

The marks you're giving away

These cost marks a student had already earned — and every one is avoidable in seconds.
Word count
Well under loses content marks; far over wastes time and adds errors.
No paragraphs
One block of text caps organisation. One paragraph per idea.
Repetition
Saying the same idea twice adds length, not marks.
Missing features
No title on an article; no greeting/sign-off on a letter.
Rep · name the mark-loss and the fix

What's costing the mark?

1. A 260-word article when the task said 120–160. problem? fix?
2. An article submitted with no headline. problem? fix?
3. Three paragraphs that each make the same point. problem? fix?
4. A friendly email that ends with no sign-off. problem? fix?
Answers: 1 over-length → cut to range · 2 missing feature → add a title · 3 repetition → replace two with new ideas · 4 missing feature → add "Speak soon, [name]".
Clinic 5 · The summary response

Select, reword, connect

The trap
Lifting whole sentences from the text, or padding the summary with examples, opinion and repetition that earn nothing.
The fix · three moves
Select only the points the question asks for; reword each in your own words; connect them into one flowing paragraph with linkers.
Leave out examples, statistics and your own opinion — a summary is points only, tightly linked.
Rep · select → reword → connect

Summarise the benefits of the scheme

Source: "The colour-coded bins made recycling simple, so more waste was sorted correctly. Because less went to landfill, the school's collection costs fell sharply. Pupils also reported feeling proud that their school was doing something visible about the environment."
Select the 3 points → ______ / ______ / ______
Reword + connect into one sentence → ______
Model: points = easier sorting · lower costs · pupil pride. Connected: "The scheme made sorting easier, which cut collection costs and gave pupils a sense of pride." Reworded, linked, no lifting.
Clinic 6 · Proofreading

The last three minutes are for errors

The trap
Spending the final minutes cramming in one more idea — and leaving a page of avoidable slips that pull down the accuracy mark.
The fix · hunt your own errors
Proof for the mistakes you make: verb endings (-s), a/the, tense consistency, spelling of the task's key words. One targeted pass beats reading it "for feel".
Keep a personal error list. If you always drop the -s on he/she verbs, that is the one thing to hunt first.
Rep · find and fix five errors

Proof this paragraph

"Dear Editor, I am writing about the bus service, which affect students every day. Last week the bus arrive late twice. This is a unacceptable situation. Many student misses their first lesson, and last month I will lose valuable time."
Answers: affect → affects (verb ending) · arrive → arrived (tense) · a → an (article) · student misses → students miss (agreement) · will lose → lost (tense). Five marks, three minutes.
