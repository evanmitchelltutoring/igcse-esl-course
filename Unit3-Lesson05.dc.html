Teaching 1 · copy this into your notes

Linking cause to effect

because  ·  since  ·  as
effect, because cause  →  I sleep badly because I scroll late.
so  ·  therefore  ·  as a result
cause, so effect  →  I scroll late, so I sleep badly.
can lead to  ·  results in  ·  causes
cause can lead to effect  →  Late scrolling can lead to poor sleep.
this means that  ·  which means
cause. This means that effect  →  I sleep badly. This means I can't focus.

Practice 1 · join cause and effect

Add a connector — keep the meaning

1. He skips breakfast ______ he loses focus by mid-morning.
2. Mild dehydration ______ tiredness and headaches.
3. She kept a steady bedtime. ______ she woke before her alarm.
4. A short walk sharpens concentration, ______ revision goes faster afterwards.
Stuck? → ask "which part is the cause?" — the connector points from it to the effect.
Push further → rewrite Q1 using a different connector without changing the meaning.
Answer · mark your own

Join cause and effect

1. He skips breakfast, so he loses focus by mid-morning. (or …focus because he skips breakfast)
2. Mild dehydration can lead to tiredness and headaches. (or results in / causes)
3. She kept a steady bedtime. As a result, she woke before her alarm. (or Therefore,)
4. A short walk sharpens concentration, which means revision goes faster afterwards. (or so)

Practice 2 · make it advice

Rewrite each as advice — use the structure named

1. "Drink more water."  → It's worth -ing   ______
2. "Don't rely on energy drinks."  → You should…   ______
3. "Put your phone in another room."  → Why don't you…?   ______

Stuck? → the structures are on the teaching slide — copy the shape, change the words.
Push further → add a reason to each with "because…".
Answer · mark your own

Make it advice

1. It's worth drinking more water. (-ing form after "worth")
2. You should not rely on energy drinks. (bare verb after "should")
3. Why don't you put your phone in another room?
4. One simple step is to take short breaks when revising.

Extra practice · spot the error

Find and fix one mistake in each

Cause–effect & advice · because / so / therefore · should / ought to / had better

1. She feels calm because of she does yoga.
2. I felt dizzy, so therefore I sat down.
3. You should to eat breakfast every day.
4. He ought exercise more often.
5. You had better not to skip meals.
6. Because she felt unwell, so she stayed home.
Spot the error · answers

Corrected

1. She feels calm because she does yoga. (because + clause)
2. I felt dizzy, so I sat down. (not so + therefore)
3. You should eat breakfast every day. (no to)
4. He ought to exercise more often.
5. You had better not skip meals. (no to)
6. Because she felt unwell, she stayed home. (drop so)

Extra practice · transform

Make a correct sentence from each prompt

1. Join with because: “He was tired. He went to bed.”
2. Join with so: “She felt stressed. She went for a walk.”
3. Join with therefore: “Sugar harms teeth. You should limit it.”
4. Advice with should: (you / drink / more water)
5. Advice with ought to: (we / eat / less salt)
6. Warning with had better: (you / not / miss / your medicine)
Build your own → I feel ___ because ___ . / I was ___ , so ___ . / To stay healthy, you should ___ . / You had better not ___ .
Transform · answers

Model sentences

1. He went to bed because he was tired.
2. She felt stressed, so she went for a walk.
3. Sugar harms your teeth; therefore, you should limit it.
4. You should drink more water.
5. We ought to eat less salt.
6. You had better not miss your medicine.
