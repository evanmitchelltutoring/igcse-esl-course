How a Trend Travels

Read · then read again

Five years ago, almost no one at my school wore wide-legged jeans. Now, on any morning, half the corridor is in them. I started to wonder how a single look spreads so fast — and why it fades just as quickly.

A trend usually begins small. A designer shows something unusual on a runway, or a teenager in one city films a thirty-second clip of an outfit. Most of these ideas go nowhere. But every so often one of them is picked up by an influencer with millions of followers, and within days it is everywhere. Last spring, a student in Seoul posted a video of herself styling an oversized blazer with shorts. The clip went viral. Three weeks later, shops in Bangkok were selling almost the same blazer, and my classmates were wearing it without knowing where it came from.

This is the strange thing about modern fashion: a look can travel from one bedroom to a hundred countries before anyone can explain why it matters. Social media has made the journey faster than ever. In the past, a style might take a year to move from a fashion capital to an ordinary high street. Today it can happen in a fortnight.

Speed has a cost, though. The faster a trend rises, the faster it falls. By the time a look becomes truly mainstream — sold cheaply in every shop, worn by everyone — the people who started it have usually moved on. What felt fresh in March can feel tired by September. Some styles return years later as a revival, dressed up as something new, and the cycle begins again.

So the next time you notice everyone suddenly wearing the same thing, it is worth asking two questions. Where did this come from? And how long before it disappears? The answers say a lot — not only about clothes, but about how quickly we copy one another.
