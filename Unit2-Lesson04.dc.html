Four Great Journeys

Reading · matching

A · The Arctic tern. No animal travels further. Each year the Arctic tern flies from the Arctic, where it breeds, all the way to the seas around Antarctica and back — a round trip of around 70,000 kilometres. Because it follows the summer at both ends of the planet, it sees more daylight than any other creature on Earth. It rarely lands, snatching fish from the surface of the sea as it goes.

B · The monarch butterfly. The monarch's journey is remarkable not for its length but for who completes it. No single butterfly makes the whole trip from Canada to the forests of central Mexico. Instead the migration is shared across several generations: the butterflies that finally arrive in Mexico are the great-grandchildren of those that first set out. How they find a forest none of them has ever seen remains one of nature's great puzzles.

C · The humpback whale. Humpback whales swim from the cold, food-rich waters near the poles to warm tropical seas where their calves are born. They make the journey without eating at all, living off the fat they built up over the summer. Along the way the males sing long, complex songs that travel for many kilometres underwater; the same song spreads from group to group until whole oceans seem to share a single tune.

D · The wildebeest. On the plains of East Africa more than a million wildebeest move in a vast circle, always chasing the rain and the fresh grass it brings. There is no leader; the herd simply turns as one when the weather changes. The most dangerous moments come at the rivers, where crocodiles wait, and only the animals that cross quickly and together are likely to survive.

Skill · answering a matching task

Question first, then scan A–D

Rule 1 · Read the question first
Find the key idea in the question (a distance? a danger? a group?), then hunt for it in the four options.
Rule 2 · Letters can repeat
"You may use each letter more than once." Don't cross options off — the same animal can be the answer twice.

You may use each letter more than once

1. Which animal makes its journey without eating? ___
2. Which animal's migration is completed across several generations? ___
3. Which animal travels in a huge group with no leader? ___

Grammar · comparing journeys

Comparative & superlative

Compare two: -er / more … than
"further than", "more daylight than"
Top of the group: the -est / the most
"the most dangerous moments"
Complete with the correct form
a) The tern flies ____ (far) than any other bird.
b) It sees ____ (much) daylight than any creature on Earth.
c) The river crossing is ____ (dangerous) moment of all.
Answers: a) further / farther · b) more · c) the most dangerous
