The rule · copy this into your notes

Two tools: hedge, and join

Hedging (be cautious)
may · might · tend to · arguably · in many cases · it seems
Rich nations arguably owe the most.
Subordination (join & weigh)
although · while · because · even though · whereas
Although they owe most, all must act.
Watch it: don't stack two hedges ("it may arguably possibly") — one is enough · a subordinate clause with although needs a main clause too ("Although it's hard" is not a sentence on its own).
show me
See the lift · weak vs strong

Absolute, then fair & connected

Weak
"Rich countries caused the problem. They must fix everything. Poor countries can do nothing."
Absolute, blaming, disconnected.
Strong
"Wealthier nations arguably owe the most, because they built their wealth first. Even so, every country can contribute something."
A hedge + subordination = fair and weighed.

Practice 1 · fix each sentence

Too absolute, or badly joined — fix it

1. Recycling solves the climate crisis. (too absolute — hedge it)
2. Although it is difficult. We must try. (join into one sentence)
3. It may possibly arguably help a little. (one hedge only)
4. Everyone always wastes food. (too absolute — soften)
5. Rich nations owe most. Poorer ones should act too. (join with while/although)
Support → ask: is it too sure (hedge) or two ideas jammed together (join)?
Stretch → rewrite one sentence using both a hedge and a subordinator.
Answer · mark your own

Fix it (sample answers)

1. Recycling can help, but it does not solve the climate crisis. (hedge)
2. Although it is difficult, we must try. (one sentence)
3. It may help a little. (one hedge)
4. Many people tend to waste food. (soften)
5. While rich nations owe most, poorer ones should act too. (join)
