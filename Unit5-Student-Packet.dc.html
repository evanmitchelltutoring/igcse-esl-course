1 · Vocabulary reference

The unit's words, grouped in four families. Say them, then use them in your writing and speaking.

Made by a Machine
algorithm — step-by-step rules a computer follows to solve a problem
artificial — made by people or machines, not occurring naturally
dataset — a large collection of information used to train a model
generate — to produce or create something
mimic — to copy the way something looks or behaves
output — the thing a process produces
prompt — the words you type to tell an AI what to make
novelty — the quality of being new; a new thing whose appeal may not last
Whose Hand, Whose Eye?
authentic — real and genuine, not fake or copied
craft — skill in making things by hand; to make with care
evoke — to bring a feeling or memory into someone's mind
express — to show a thought or feeling through words or art
genuine — real and sincere; truly what it claims to be
intention — the aim or purpose behind doing something
intuition — sensing something without conscious reasoning
soulless — lacking warmth, feeling, or character
The Style Thief
attribution — naming who created or is responsible for a work
compensation — money paid to make up for loss or harm
consent — permission for something to happen
derivative — copied from or based on something else; not original
distinctive — clearly different in a way easy to recognise
imitate — to copy the way someone does something
infringe — to break a rule or law, especially about rights
scrape — to collect data automatically from across the internet
Real Art, Real Prize?
controversy — strong public disagreement about something
credibility — the quality of being believable and trusted
disqualify — to remove someone from a competition for breaking a rule
judgement — the ability to form a careful opinion or decision
legitimate — reasonable and acceptable; lawful
merit — the quality of being good and deserving praise
recognition — public acknowledgement that something is good
transparency — being open and honest, so others can see how things are done
Academic argument chunks — for essays & debates
it could be argued that — hedge a claim you are not stating as certain
there is no denying that — concede a strong point before answering it
this calls into question — challenge or cast doubt on a claim
the heart of the matter is — signal your central point or verdict
Word families — learn one, gain three

One root grows into several. Learn the family and you always have the right form for a sentence.

generate → generation → generative
imitate → imitation → imitative
create → creativity → creative
First-language bridge

In your first language, note what these key ideas mean — it helps them stick: intention · derivative · transparency.

5 · Grammar (Use of English)

5a · Modals of speculation & deduction (must / might / could / can't)

Choose the modal that shows how sure you are. must = almost certain yes · can't = almost certain no · might/could = possible.

  1. The style is exactly hers, so the program ____ have studied her work.
  2. It looks handmade, but it ____ be AI — I really can't tell.
  3. A machine feels nothing, so it ____ truly mean what it makes.
  4. The colours are so unusual — a person ____ have chosen them on purpose.
  5. Rewrite as speculation: "I'm sure the artist edited it." → It ____
5b · Concession linkers (although / even though / while / despite / however)
  1. ____ the image is beautiful, it was made without consent.
  2. The output is impressive; ____, no artist was paid for it.
  3. ____ being new, AI art relies entirely on older human work.
  4. Join with while: "AI can copy a style. It cannot invent one from nothing."

6 · Writing

6a · A persuasive speech — "Should our art prize accept AI entries?" (150–200 words)
Shape: Hook + stance → Grant → Counter → Stand → Call to action.   Stems: "Ask yourself this…" · "There is no denying that…" · "But in reality…" · "So when you vote…". Speak to the audience directly; use a concession linker and 3+ target words (legitimate · merit · credibility · there is no denying that).
6b · An argumentative essay — "A machine can never truly make art." How far do you agree? (120–160 words)
Shape: Stance → Concession (grant–counter–stand) → Reasoning (evidence → speculation → verdict) → Your strongest argument → Conclusion.   Grade your certainty: could / might → must / can't. Use concession linkers and 5+ target words (it could be argued that · this calls into question · intention · authentic · legitimate · merit).
