Exam skill · before we listen

Listen for the signal words

A careful speaker signals how sure they are. The little words around a claim tell you whether to write it down as fact, rumour, or doubt.
Fact
"The ship was found adrift." Stated plainly, with a date or place.
Rumour
"Reportedly… it is said that…" A claim passed along.
Doubt
"Most researchers believe… probably…" A hedged judgement.

Listen, then answer

1. 1930, in northern Canada (the village of Angikuni).
2. Any two: smouldering fires · half-eaten food · starved sled dogs still tethered.
3. No official records of a search and no reliable newspaper accounts from the time.
4. That it was exaggerated or invented — likely a myth, not a real event.

Signal words "reportedly / according to the story / most researchers believe" mark this as rumour, then doubt — never stated fact.

Track 2 · answers

Listen, then answer

1. The Mary Celeste, found in December 1872.
2. In good condition — cargo mostly untouched, food and water still aboard.
3. The lifeboat was missing — they seem to have left on purpose.
4. Fear of an explosion from alcohol fumes — they abandoned ship and were lost.

The opening is fact (date, plainly stated); the explanation is doubt ("most historians favour", "perhaps").
